# 00.10 — Document Governance

**Document ID:** 00.10  
**Document class:** Constitutional  
**Status:** Approved  
**Directory:** `00-CONSTITUTION/`

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the principles, rules and governance framework governing the creation, organization, review, approval, maintenance, revision, preservation and retirement of Veyra's documentation.

Documentation is a foundational component of Veyra's engineering process. It provides the authoritative record of the project's identity, objectives, requirements, constraints, architecture, decisions, implementation expectations and operational knowledge.

Veyra must treat documentation as a governed engineering asset rather than informal supporting material.

## 2. Foundational Documentation Principles

Veyra's documentation system shall follow these principles:

- **Accuracy:** Documents must represent information as accurately as reasonably possible.
- **Consistency:** Documents must not contradict established governing principles without an explicit resolution.
- **Traceability:** Important requirements, decisions and changes must be traceable to their sources and consequences.
- **Clarity:** Documents must communicate their purpose, scope and meaning clearly.
- **Maintainability:** Documentation must remain understandable and maintainable as Veyra evolves.
- **Accountability:** Document creation, review, approval and modification must be attributable.
- **Integrity:** Authoritative documents must be protected against unauthorized modification or destruction.
- **Accessibility:** Relevant documentation must be discoverable by authorized humans and engineering processes.
- **Proportionality:** Documentation requirements should reflect the significance, complexity and risk of the subject.
- **Continuity:** Important knowledge must not depend exclusively on undocumented individual knowledge or temporary conversational context.

## 3. Documentation as an Engineering Asset

Documentation shall be treated as part of Veyra's engineering foundation.

It must support:

- Project understanding and continuity.
- Requirements definition and validation.
- Architectural reasoning.
- Engineering implementation.
- Testing and verification.
- Operational procedures.
- Security and governance.
- Decision-making and accountability.
- Maintenance and future development.
- Knowledge preservation and transfer.

Where documentation is necessary to understand, reproduce, verify, operate or maintain a system, its absence or inaccuracy must be treated as an engineering concern.

Documentation must evolve alongside the systems and processes it describes.

## 4. Documentation Hierarchy

Veyra's documentation shall be organized into a defined hierarchy.

The hierarchy establishes the relationship between foundational authority, formal requirements, architectural decisions, technical specifications, implementation records and operational knowledge.

The proposed hierarchy is:

1. **Constitutional documentation:** Establishes Veyra's identity, mission, boundaries, principles and governing constraints.
2. **Governance documentation:** Defines how the project, its decisions, authority and controlled assets are governed.
3. **Requirements documentation:** Defines what Veyra must achieve and the conditions it must satisfy.
4. **Architectural documentation:** Defines the structural organization and major technical design decisions.
5. **Design and specification documentation:** Defines detailed technical behavior, interfaces, components and contracts.
6. **Implementation documentation:** Describes actual implementation, dependencies, configuration and code organization.
7. **Verification documentation:** Defines and records testing, validation, evidence and acceptance.
8. **Operational documentation:** Describes deployment, operation, monitoring, recovery and maintenance.
9. **Research and decision records:** Preserve investigations, alternatives, evidence, assumptions and technical decisions.

Lower-level documents must remain consistent with higher-level governing documents.

The hierarchy must not prevent legitimate feedback from implementation, testing and operations from identifying weaknesses or necessary changes in higher-level documentation.

## 5. Document Classification

Every formal Veyra document must have an identifiable classification appropriate to its purpose.

Proposed classifications include:

- Constitutional.
- Governance.
- Requirements.
- Architecture.
- Technical specification.
- Design.
- Implementation.
- Verification and testing.
- Operations and maintenance.
- Research.
- Decision record.
- Reference material.

A document may contain information relevant to multiple categories, but its primary classification must be clear.

Classification must help establish the document's intended authority, lifecycle and relationship with other project materials.

## 6. Document Identity and Metadata

Formal documents must have sufficient identifying information to support discovery, interpretation and governance.

Depending on document type, metadata should include:

- Document title.
- Unique document identifier.
- Filename.
- Classification.
- Version.
- Status.
- Creation date.
- Last modification date.
- Author or responsible party.
- Reviewer or approving authority, where applicable.
- Related documents.
- Relevant change history.

Not every metadata field must appear in every document, but sufficient information must exist to establish its identity, status and authority.

Document identifiers must remain stable when filenames or content are revised, unless a formal document-governance decision establishes otherwise.

## 7. Document Creation

Formal documentation must be created through a deliberate process.

A document should have:

- A defined purpose.
- An identified scope.
- A clear relationship to the project.
- An appropriate classification.
- A responsible author or originating authority.
- Relevant supporting evidence or references.
- An identifiable review and approval path.

Documents must not be created merely to increase repository size or produce unnecessary administrative complexity.

Their existence must serve a legitimate project, governance, engineering, operational or knowledge-preservation purpose.

## 8. Review and Approval

Formal documents must undergo review appropriate to their significance and classification.

Review should evaluate:

- Accuracy.
- Completeness.
- Internal consistency.
- Compatibility with governing documents.
- Technical validity, where applicable.
- Clarity and interpretation.
- Security and governance implications.
- Missing requirements or unresolved assumptions.
- Relationships and dependencies with other documents.

Documents requiring formal approval must not be treated as authoritative merely because they have been drafted, uploaded or committed to version control.

Approval authority must be established through the applicable governance framework.

The approval process must distinguish between drafting, reviewing, approving, publishing and implementing a document.

## 9. Document Status

Formal documents must have an identifiable status.

The project should support statuses such as:

- **Proposed:** A document or change has been suggested but is not yet formally accepted.
- **Draft:** Content is being developed and remains subject to review.
- **Under Review:** Content is undergoing formal examination.
- **Approved:** Content has received the required authorization.
- **Active:** An approved document is currently in force or applicable.
- **Superseded:** A newer authoritative document or revision has replaced it.
- **Deprecated:** A document remains historically available but is no longer recommended for use.
- **Archived:** A document has been retained for historical or reference purposes.

The exact relationship between approval and active status must be established by the applicable document lifecycle process.

A document's status must never be misrepresented.

## 10. Version Control and Change History

Formal documents must be subject to controlled version management.

Changes must be traceable and should preserve sufficient historical information to establish:

- What changed.
- When the change occurred.
- Why the change was necessary.
- Who or what initiated it.
- Who reviewed or approved it.
- Which version was affected.
- Whether the change affected dependent documents.

Version control systems should be used where appropriate to preserve document history, compare revisions and support recovery.

Historical versions must not be silently overwritten when doing so would destroy important governance or engineering evidence.

## 11. Document Amendment and Revision

Documents may be amended when new evidence, requirements, decisions, defects or changes in project direction justify revision.

Amendments must follow an appropriate review and approval process.

A revision must consider:

- The original purpose of the document.
- The reason for the proposed change.
- The effect on existing requirements and decisions.
- Dependencies on other documents.
- Backward compatibility, where relevant.
- Implementation and operational consequences.
- Whether additional approval is necessary.

Changes affecting constitutional principles, authority boundaries or foundational project identity must receive the level of human approval required by the constitutional governance framework.

## 12. Document Consistency and Conflict Resolution

Veyra's documentation must be maintained as a coherent body of knowledge.

Where documents conflict, the conflict must be identified, assessed and resolved through the appropriate governance process.

Resolution should consider:

- Document classification and authority.
- Approval and active status.
- Scope and applicability.
- Specificity of the statements involved.
- Relevant dependencies.
- Established constitutional constraints.
- Supporting evidence and subsequent decisions.

A lower-level document must not silently override a higher-level governing document.

Where the correct interpretation cannot be established, affected decisions or activities must be escalated rather than based on arbitrary assumptions.

## 13. Traceability and Cross-Document Relationships

Important documents must maintain traceable relationships with relevant project materials.

Examples include:

- Constitutional principles linked to requirements.
- Requirements linked to architectural decisions.
- Architectural decisions linked to design specifications.
- Design specifications linked to implementation.
- Requirements linked to verification evidence.
- Operational procedures linked to deployed components.

Traceability should support the ability to determine:

- Why a requirement exists.
- Which governing principle supports it.
- Where it is implemented.
- How it is verified.
- What may be affected if it changes.

Traceability must be maintained proportionately to the complexity and significance of the subject.

## 14. Documentation and Implementation Alignment

Documentation must accurately distinguish between intended, approved, proposed and implemented system behavior.

Veyra must not assume that an approved design has been implemented.

Likewise, the existence of implementation code does not automatically establish that the implementation has been formally approved or verified.

Material discrepancies between documentation and actual implementation must be identified and resolved.

Where implementation differs from an approved specification, the difference must be documented, evaluated and addressed through the appropriate change process.

Documentation must not be manipulated merely to make an unapproved implementation appear compliant.

## 15. Documentation Integrity and Protection

Authoritative documentation must be protected against unauthorized modification, fabrication, deletion or concealment.

The project must preserve the integrity of:

- Constitutional documents.
- Approved requirements.
- Architectural decisions.
- Security and governance specifications.
- Verification evidence.
- Operational records.
- Material change histories.

Access to documents and their modification must respect applicable permissions and confidentiality requirements.

The ability to edit a document must not automatically confer authority to approve its contents.

## 16. Document Ownership and Responsibility

Formal documents must have an identifiable responsible party or governing authority.

Responsibility may include:

- Maintaining accuracy.
- Coordinating reviews.
- Identifying outdated information.
- Managing proposed revisions.
- Preserving relevant history.
- Maintaining cross-document consistency.
- Ensuring appropriate approval.
- Communicating material changes.

Document responsibility must not be confused with unrestricted authority to change or approve a document.

The precise assignment of roles and responsibilities shall be defined through the project's governance arrangements.

## 17. Archiving, Deprecation and Supersession

Documents that are no longer active must be handled through a controlled lifecycle process.

A document may be superseded when a newer approved document replaces it.

A document may be deprecated when it is no longer recommended for use but still has historical or reference value.

A document may be archived when it is retained primarily for historical, legal, audit or knowledge-preservation purposes.

Superseded or archived documents must not be confused with current authoritative documents.

Where appropriate, a replacement document must identify the document it supersedes and explain the relevant transition.

Historical records must be preserved in accordance with applicable retention, privacy, security and legal requirements.

## 18. Repository and Publication Governance

The Veyra repository shall serve as an important controlled source of project documentation and history.

Repository operations must support:

- Controlled changes.
- Reviewable differences.
- Meaningful commit history.
- Appropriate access permissions.
- Recovery of previous versions.
- Protection of authoritative documents.
- Clear identification of published changes.

A successful commit or push establishes that a change has been recorded in version control; it does not independently establish formal document approval.

Publishing documentation and approving its contents are distinct governance activities.

The repository's structure and maintenance practices must support long-term project continuity.

## 19. Documentation of Research and Decisions

Research findings, technical evaluations, assumptions and significant decisions should be documented when they materially influence Veyra's direction or implementation.

Decision records should preserve, where relevant:

- The problem or question.
- Context and constraints.
- Alternatives considered.
- Evidence and evaluation criteria.
- The decision and its rationale.
- Known limitations and consequences.
- Approval or decision authority.
- Conditions that may justify reconsideration.

Research must distinguish established evidence from speculation, assumptions and unresolved questions.

Rejected alternatives may be retained where their rationale could prevent repeated investigation or explain historical decisions.

## 20. Documentation Automation and AI-Assisted Changes

Veyra may use automated processes and AI capabilities to create, analyze, validate, update and organize documentation.

Such capabilities must operate within the applicable authority and approval boundaries.

Automated documentation processes must not independently:

- Approve documents beyond their delegated authority.
- Change constitutional authority.
- Conceal material changes.
- Fabricate sources, evidence or approval.
- Silently override authoritative documents.
- Remove important historical information without authorization.

AI-generated content must receive appropriate validation before being treated as authoritative.

Automation should improve documentation quality, consistency and maintainability without weakening human governance.

## 21. Documentation Maintenance and Quality

Documentation must be maintained throughout the project lifecycle.

Maintenance should address:

- Outdated information.
- Broken references.
- Missing relationships.
- Contradictions.
- Incomplete explanations.
- Obsolete technical instructions.
- Changes in implementation.
- Changes in requirements or operating conditions.

Documentation quality should be evaluated by its usefulness, correctness, clarity, completeness and maintainability rather than its length.

The project should avoid unnecessary duplication and should establish clear authoritative sources for information that would otherwise be repeated.

## 22. Relationship with Other Constitutional Documents

This document must be interpreted consistently with the other Veyra constitutional documents.

In particular:

- **Project Identity:** Documentation must preserve Veyra's established identity.
- **Vision and Mission:** Documentation must support the project's intended purpose.
- **Scope and Boundaries:** Documents must remain within the project's defined scope.
- **Core Principles:** Documentation practices must reinforce foundational principles.
- **Definitions and Terminology:** Formal terminology must remain consistent.
- **Veyra–AION Relationship:** Documentation must preserve the independence and distinction between Veyra and AION.
- **Baseline Requirements and Constraints:** Requirements and constraints must be accurately represented and traceable.
- **Development Philosophy:** Documentation must support deliberate, evidence-based and governed development.
- **Governance and Human Authority:** Document authority, approval and amendment must remain subject to legitimate human governance.

## 23. Constitutional Inviolability

The principles established in this document are binding at the constitutional level.

They must not be weakened or bypassed merely to accelerate development, simplify administration or increase automation.

Changes to this document must follow the project's formal document-governance process.

No autonomous process may independently alter the constitutional documentation framework to expand its own authority or bypass established approval requirements.

## 24. Final Constitutional Statement

Veyra shall maintain documentation as a governed, traceable and enduring engineering asset.

Its documentation shall accurately represent its identity, requirements, architecture, decisions, implementation and operational state.

Authoritative documents shall be identifiable, reviewable, approved through legitimate processes and protected throughout their lifecycle.

Changes shall be deliberate, traceable and consistent with the constitutional hierarchy.

Documentation shall preserve institutional knowledge, support engineering correctness, enable meaningful oversight and provide continuity as Veyra evolves.

**The integrity of Veyra's documentation is essential to the integrity, accountability and continuity of Veyra itself.**
