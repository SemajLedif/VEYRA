# VEYRA — Scope & Boundaries

**Document ID:** VEYRA-00.3  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Canonical Working Document  
**Parent:** Phase 0 — Foundation / Constitution  
**Depends On:** VEYRA-00.1 Project Identity, VEYRA-00.2 Vision & Mission

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the canonical scope and boundaries of Veyra.

It defines:

- what Veyra is intended to encompass;
- what capabilities fall within its mission;
- what Veyra is not intended to be;
- the boundaries between Veyra and related systems or projects;
- the distinction between present development scope and eventual capability scope.

It does not define the final technical architecture or implementation technology.

## 2. Scope Principle

Veyra's scope is defined by the software-engineering problem it is intended to solve, rather than by a fixed collection of technologies, agents, models, or implementation components.

The scope should therefore be broad enough to support general-purpose software engineering while remaining precise enough to distinguish Veyra from unrelated forms of artificial intelligence.

## 3. Primary Scope

Veyra encompasses the capabilities required to support software production across the software lifecycle.

The intended scope includes:

```text
Idea
  ↓
Discovery
  ↓
Research
  ↓
Requirements
  ↓
Planning
  ↓
Architecture
  ↓
Design
  ↓
Implementation
  ↓
Testing
  ↓
Verification
  ↓
Debugging
  ↓
Security
  ↓
Deployment
  ↓
Operations
  ↓
Maintenance
  ↓
Evolution
```

These stages represent the intended engineering domain of Veyra.

They do not imply that every stage must be fully autonomous in the first implementation.

- understanding existing codebases;

- understanding technical constraints;
- understanding project history and decisions.

- researching implementation approaches;
- researching libraries and frameworks;
- researching architecture patterns;
- researching security considerations;
- researching relevant technical knowledge.

### 4.3 Planning

- decomposing objectives;
- creating engineering plans;
- identifying dependencies;

- coordinating parallel activities.

### 4.4 Design

- system architecture;

- component design;

## 5. General Technology Scope

Veyra is intended to work across diverse technology stacks.

Its scope is therefore not restricted to:

- one programming language;
- one framework;
- one operating system;
- one database;
- one cloud provider;
- one deployment platform;
- one model provider;
- one AI model;
- one agent framework.

Specific technologies will be selected through later research and architectural decisions.

## 6. General Project Scope

Veyra is intended to support arbitrary software projects to the extent that its capabilities and resources permit.

Projects may differ in:

- purpose;
- domain;
- scale;
- complexity;
- technology;
- architecture;
- infrastructure;
- security requirements;
- compliance requirements;
- deployment environment;
- operational requirements.

Veyra must therefore be designed as an adaptable engineering system rather than a single-purpose application builder.

## 7. Intelligence Scope

Veyra's scope includes the coordination of different forms of software-engineering intelligence.

These may include:

- reasoning;
- coding;
- research;
- planning;
- architecture;
- testing;
- security analysis;
- debugging;
- operational analysis;
- documentation;
- domain-specific expertise.

The implementation may use multiple models, specialized workers, tools, or other intelligence resources.

The exact mechanism remains undecided.

## 8. Workforce Scope

Veyra's intended scope includes dynamic engineering workforce formation.

The system should be capable of composing the capabilities required for a particular project rather than depending on a permanently fixed collection of named agents.

Conceptually:

```text
Project Requirements
        ↓
Required Capabilities
        ↓
Roles
        ↓
Workers
        ↓
Teams
        ↓
Coordinated Engineering
```

There is no canonical fixed numerical limit on the number of workers.

The previously used "100 engineers" concept is illustrative of scale and coordinated capability rather than a literal system boundary.

## 9. Tool and Execution Scope

Veyra's engineering scope includes interaction with tools and execution environments necessary to perform software engineering.

Potential categories include:

- source-control systems;
- repositories;
- development environments;
- code execution;
- testing systems;
- package managers;
- build systems;
- deployment systems;
- infrastructure;
- observability systems;
- research systems;
- external software tools.

Access to such resources must be governed by permissions and security controls.

The existence of a tool category within scope does not establish a specific implementation.

## 10. Project State and Knowledge Scope

Veyra's scope includes maintaining and using relevant engineering knowledge and project state.

This may include:

- requirements;
- source code;
- architecture;
- specifications;
- plans;
- tasks;
- decisions;
- test results;
- documentation;
- configuration;
- project history;
- engineering knowledge.

The exact distinction between memory, knowledge, state, context, and persistent project artifacts remains subject to later research and architecture.

## 11. Research Scope

Research is within Veyra's core scope rather than being an optional external activity.

Veyra should eventually be capable of researching problems relevant to the project before making engineering decisions.

However, Veyra is not defined as a general-purpose research system for every domain of human knowledge.

Its research capability is primarily scoped toward:

> **Knowledge required to perform software engineering and achieve project objectives.**

The system may use broader information where necessary.

## 12. Security Scope

Security is within Veyra's core engineering scope.

Security considerations include:

- software security;
- dependency security;
- secrets management;
- access control;
- agent permissions;
- execution isolation;
- infrastructure security;
- deployment security;
- supply-chain considerations;
- protection against malicious or unsafe actions.

Security is not a feature that is intended to be added only after implementation.

It is a foundational engineering concern.

## 13. Human Interaction Scope

Veyra includes interaction with humans where appropriate.

Humans may:

- define objectives;
- provide requirements;
- establish constraints;
- review work;
- approve sensitive actions;
- resolve ambiguous decisions;
- intervene in execution;
- govern system evolution.

Veyra's scope therefore includes human-directed, human-supervised, and increasingly autonomous engineering workflows.

The exact autonomy model remains subject to later design.

## 14. Autonomy Boundary

Autonomy is a capability within Veyra's scope.

Unrestricted autonomy is not established as a requirement.

Veyra must operate within:

- defined permissions;
- explicit authority;
- security boundaries;
- project constraints;
- applicable governance;
- appropriate human intervention mechanisms.

The goal is controlled autonomy rather than uncontrolled access.

## 15. Veyra and AION Boundary

AION is outside Veyra's fundamental product scope.

Veyra is not an AION-specific system.

AION may be:

- a future project built using Veyra;
- a customer-like project;
- an internal project;
- an important validation target.

But AION does not define Veyra's general architecture or limit its software-engineering scope.

## 16. Veyra and the Builder Foundry Boundary

The Builder Foundry and Veyra are related but distinct.

**Builder Foundry**

The environment, methodology, and supporting ecosystem used to build Veyra.

**Veyra**

The software-engineering intelligence/platform being constructed.

The Foundry may contain tools and processes that are not part of Veyra's eventual customer-facing product.

Conversely, Veyra may eventually provide capabilities that become useful for improving the Foundry itself.

The exact boundary may evolve as the project develops, but the concepts should remain distinguishable.

## 17. Customer Boundary

Veyra is intended eventually to support external customers.

Customer projects are within the operational scope of Veyra's eventual product.

However, customer data, customer projects, customer permissions, and customer environments must remain appropriately isolated.

The exact tenancy, isolation, billing, and customer architecture remain undecided.

## 18. Commercial Boundary

Commercialization is within the strategic scope of Veyra.

However, commercial mechanisms are not part of the foundational definition of the engineering intelligence itself.

For example:

- pricing;
- billing;
- subscriptions;
- usage accounting;
- customer plans;
- commercial packaging;

may surround Veyra without defining its core intelligence.

These subjects will receive dedicated treatment in later phases.

## 19. Development Scope Versus Ultimate Scope

A critical distinction must be maintained between:

**Development Scope**

What we can realistically build, test, and operate at the current stage of the project.

**Ultimate Capability Scope**

What Veyra is intended to become if the underlying technical challenges can be solved.

The current implementation must not be mistaken for the final definition of Veyra.

Likewise, the ambitious long-term vision must not be treated as evidence that the capability already exists.

## 20. Initial Development Boundary

The initial development effort must remain constrained by available:

- compute;
- models;
- tools;
- infrastructure;
- knowledge;
- engineering time;
- budget;
- testing capability.

The project maintains a deliberate objective of a viable $0 or very-low-cost development path during early construction.

This is a development constraint, not a permanent requirement that production Veyra operate without paid resources.

## 21. Architectural Boundary

This document intentionally does not define the final architecture.

It does not establish:

- specific services;
- specific agents;
- specific databases;
- specific frameworks;
- specific communication protocols;
- specific model providers;
- specific orchestration technologies;
- specific cloud infrastructure.

Those decisions belong to later research and architecture stages.

## 22. Capability Expansion

Veyra's scope may expand when engineering research establishes that a new capability is necessary to fulfill the software-engineering mission.

Such expansion must be:

1. explicitly identified;
2. documented;
3. evaluated against existing scope;
4. checked for overlap;
5. incorporated through the appropriate governance process.

Scope expansion must not occur merely because a technology is interesting.

## 23. Scope Exclusion Principle

A capability should not be considered part of Veyra merely because Veyra could technically be made to perform it.

The relevant question is:

> **Does the capability materially contribute to Veyra's mission of general-purpose software engineering and software production?**

If not, it should remain outside the core product scope unless a later decision establishes a legitimate relationship.

## 24. Boundary Principle

> **Veyra encompasses the intelligence, coordination, tools, execution, verification, operational capabilities, and governance necessary to perform general-purpose software engineering, while remaining distinct from the individual projects it builds, the broader AION vision, and the Foundry used to construct Veyra itself.**

## 25. Status

This document establishes the canonical Phase 0 scope and boundaries for Veyra.

It intentionally leaves technical implementation and architectural choices open.

Future documents may refine these boundaries when research or architectural evidence demonstrates that refinement is necessary.

Any material change to the scope must be explicitly documented and governed rather than silently introduced.
