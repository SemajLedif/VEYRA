## PART I — FOUNDATIONS AND GOVERNING AUTHORITY

### Clause 1 — Purpose

This document establishes the constitutional principles, responsibilities, rights, safeguards and governance requirements governing Veyra's customers, users, products and services throughout their lifecycles.

### Clause 2 — Constitutional Authority

1. This document forms part of the Veyra Constitution.
2. All subordinate policies, processes, contracts, specifications and operational procedures governing customers, products and services must comply with it.
3. No commercial, technical, operational or organisational decision may override a constitutional requirement.
4. Where an apparent conflict exists, the matter must be referred through the constitutional governance and interpretation process established by 00.10.

### Clause 3 — Scope of Application

This document applies to:

1. Veyra and all of its product and service operations.
2. Products and services developed, owned, operated, distributed, licensed or maintained by Veyra.
3. AION and other systems developed within the Veyra environment.
4. Customers, users, administrators, representatives and affected individuals.
5. Employees, contractors, agents, automated systems and authorised third parties performing relevant responsibilities.
6. Every stage of product and service conception, development, delivery, operation, modification and retirement.

### Clause 4 — Foundational Customer Principle

Veyra must create and deliver legitimate value while respecting the rights, interests, safety, privacy, autonomy and dignity of the people and organisations affected by its products and services.

Revenue, growth, engagement, automation, convenience and technical achievement must never justify deception, exploitation, unacceptable harm or abandonment of constitutional obligations.

### Clause 5 — Product and Service Integrity

Every product and service must have:

1. A documented purpose and intended value.
2. An identifiable accountable owner.
3. A defined intended audience and operating environment.
4. Documented capabilities, limitations and exclusions.
5. Defined authority, permissions and operating boundaries.
6. Appropriate risk, quality and assurance arrangements.
7. Documented governance and accountability.
8. An appropriate lifecycle and retirement plan.

### Clause 6 — No Misrepresentation

Veyra must not knowingly misrepresent a product's or service's capabilities, autonomy, reliability, safety, security, privacy, performance, limitations or expected outcomes.

### Clause 7 — Evidence Over Assertion

Material product and service claims must be supported by appropriate evidence.

A claim must not be represented as verified merely because it is technically plausible, internally believed or generated by an AI system.

### Clause 8 — Proportionality

Governance controls must be proportionate to the nature, scale, complexity, criticality and risk of the relevant product or service.

Proportionality must not be used to eliminate mandatory safeguards or weaken constitutional protections.

## PART II — CUSTOMER, USER AND STAKEHOLDER GOVERNANCE

### Clause 9 — Distinction Between Customer and User

Veyra must distinguish between:

1. A customer who purchases, commissions, subscribes to or otherwise obtains a product or service.
2. A user who directly interacts with or relies on a product or service.
3. An account holder who controls an account or subscription.
4. An administrator who manages a product or service environment.
5. An authorised representative acting for another person or organisation.
6. An affected individual whose information, rights, interests or circumstances may be influenced by the product or service.
7. A stakeholder with a legitimate interest in the product or service.

These categories may overlap but must not automatically be treated as equivalent.

### Clause 10 — Recognition of Affected Individuals

Governance must consider people affected by a product or service even when they are not direct customers, account holders or registered users.

### Clause 11 — Defined Customer Relationships

Every customer relationship must have a legitimate and identifiable basis, including the applicable contractual, consent-based, organisational or other authorised relationship.

### Clause 12 — Customer Eligibility

Veyra must establish eligibility requirements where the product's purpose, risks, intended audience or applicable obligations make such controls necessary.

### Clause 13 — Identity, Authentication and Authorisation

Veyra must distinguish:

1. Identity verification: establishing or assessing who a person or organisation is.
2. Authentication: verifying that a person or system is entitled to use an account or identity.
3. Authorisation: establishing what that authenticated person or system is permitted to do.

Success in one of these processes must not automatically grant the permissions associated with another.

### Clause 14 — Representative Authority

Where a person acts on behalf of another person or organisation, Veyra must establish appropriate evidence of the representative's authority before permitting actions that depend on that authority.

### Clause 15 — Fair Customer Treatment

Customers and users must be treated fairly, consistently and respectfully.

Veyra must not exploit confusion, dependency, vulnerability, lack of technical knowledge or information asymmetry.

### Clause 16 — Accessibility and Inclusion

Products and services must consider accessibility, usability, reasonable accommodation and the differing needs and abilities of intended users.

### Clause 17 — Customer Autonomy

Customers and users must retain appropriate control over their participation, choices, information and use of Veyra products and services.

### Clause 18 — No Coercive Dependency

Veyra must not deliberately create unnecessary dependency or impose unjustified barriers to cancellation, switching, data retrieval, portability or service termination.

### Clause 19 — Stakeholder Consideration

Product and service decisions must consider legitimate stakeholder interests, including those of people who may experience indirect or unintended consequences.

## PART III — CUSTOMER RIGHTS AND PROTECTIONS

### Clause 20 — Customer Rights Framework

Veyra must establish and maintain a customer rights framework applicable to its products and services.

The framework must identify rights, relevant conditions, available remedies and the mechanisms through which rights may be exercised.

### Clause 21 — Right to Accurate Information

Customers and users must receive accurate, understandable and appropriately complete information about material aspects of products and services.

### Clause 22 — Right to Understand Material Limitations

Customers and users must be informed of limitations, exclusions, significant dependencies and foreseeable conditions that may materially affect their use of a product or service.

### Clause 23 — Right to Fair Treatment

Customers must not be subjected to deceptive, discriminatory, manipulative, exploitative or unjustifiably inconsistent treatment.

### Clause 24 — Right to Privacy

Customer and user information must be handled according to applicable privacy obligations, disclosed purposes and the safeguards established by Veyra.

### Clause 25 — Right to Appropriate Data Control

Subject to applicable law and legitimate limitations, customers and relevant individuals must have appropriate mechanisms to access, retrieve, correct, export or request deletion of information relating to them.

### Clause 26 — Right to Raise Concerns

Customers and users must have accessible means of raising complaints, reporting harmful outcomes and challenging relevant product or service behaviour.

### Clause 27 — Right to Human Review

Where a consequential automated decision materially affects a person and human review is appropriate or required, Veyra must provide an effective route to an appropriately authorised human reviewer.

### Clause 28 — Right to Understand Material Automated Involvement

Where automated or AI-driven activity materially affects a customer or user, Veyra must provide appropriate information about that involvement.

### Clause 29 — Right to Reasonable Exit

Customers must have reasonable mechanisms to cancel, terminate or leave a product or service, subject to legitimate contractual and legal requirements.

### Clause 30 — Right to Appropriate Notice

Customers must receive appropriate notice of material product or service changes, significant interruptions, relevant changes in terms and planned termination.

### Clause 31 — Right to Fair Redress

Where Veyra is responsible for a substantiated failure, customers must have access to appropriate corrective or redress mechanisms consistent with applicable obligations.

### Clause 32 — Rights Must Be Operational

Customer rights must not exist only in statements or marketing material. Veyra must establish practical mechanisms, accountable ownership and appropriate records to implement them.

### Clause 33 — No Waiver Through Confusion

Veyra must not treat a customer's failure to understand obscure, ambiguous or misleading terms as meaningful agreement to obligations that were not properly communicated.

### Clause 34 — No Retaliation for Exercising Rights

Customers, users and affected individuals must not be subjected to retaliation for exercising legitimate rights, raising good-faith concerns or submitting complaints.

## PART IV — PRODUCT AND SERVICE STRATEGY

### Clause 35 — Product Purpose

Every product must have a documented purpose explaining:

1. The problem it is intended to address.
2. Its intended audience.
3. The value it is intended to provide.
4. Its operating environment.
5. Its intended outcomes.
6. Its limitations and exclusions.

### Clause 36 — Service Purpose

Every service must have a documented purpose, scope, delivery model, ownership structure and intended service outcomes.

### Clause 37 — Product and Service Classification

Veyra must maintain a classification system that considers function, audience, operational criticality, data sensitivity, autonomy, potential harm, dependencies and relevant legal or regulatory exposure.

### Clause 38 — Product Ownership

Every product must have an identifiable and accountable product owner or formally designated ownership authority.

### Clause 39 — Service Ownership

Every operational service must have an identifiable and accountable service owner.

### Clause 40 — Product and Service Boundaries

The intended capabilities, limitations, responsibilities, exclusions, dependencies and operating boundaries of each product and service must be documented.

### Clause 41 — Portfolio Governance

Veyra must maintain oversight of its product and service portfolio.

Portfolio decisions must consider strategic alignment, customer value, feasibility, cost, risk, operational capacity, dependencies and sustainability.

### Clause 42 — Approval Before Development

No product may enter an authorised development programme without an appropriate decision establishing its purpose, ownership, preliminary feasibility and justification.

### Clause 43 — Approval Before Service Delivery

No service may be offered or operated without appropriate approval of its purpose, ownership, delivery arrangements, risks and operational readiness.

### Clause 44 — No Unjustified Expansion

Material expansion of a product's purpose, audience, autonomy, capabilities or operating environment requires reassessment and appropriate approval.

### Clause 45 — Product Viability

Veyra must assess whether it has the technical, organisational, financial and operational capacity to develop and sustain a proposed product or service responsibly.

## PART V — PRODUCT LIFECYCLE GOVERNANCE

### Clause 46 — Controlled Product Lifecycle

Every product must follow a governed lifecycle that includes, as applicable:

1. Discovery and problem definition.
2. Research and feasibility.
3. Product definition.
4. Requirements and design.
5. Development and verification.
6. Validation and acceptance.
7. Release and deployment.
8. Operation and maintenance.
9. Improvement and evolution.
10. Suspension, replacement or retirement.

### Clause 47 — Lifecycle Accountability

Accountability must be established at every lifecycle stage and maintained through transitions between teams, departments, systems and external providers.

### Clause 48 — Discovery and Problem Validation

Product development must be based on a sufficiently investigated problem or legitimate opportunity.

Verified customer needs must be distinguished from assumptions, internal preferences and unsupported market claims.

### Clause 49 — Requirements Ownership

Product requirements must have identifiable ownership, appropriate authority and documented acceptance criteria.

### Clause 50 — Requirements Changes

Material changes must be assessed for their effects on customer value, safety, privacy, security, quality, cost, service commitments and previous approvals.

### Clause 51 — Design Approval

Products must receive appropriate design review before implementation or deployment, proportionate to their complexity and risk.

### Clause 52 — Development Governance

Product development must comply with 00.13 and all other applicable constitutional requirements.

### Clause 53 — Product Validation

Products must be evaluated against their intended purpose and the needs of their intended users.

Technical functionality alone does not establish fitness for purpose.

### Clause 54 — Product Acceptance

Product acceptance must be explicit, evidence-based and performed by an authorised person or authority.

### Clause 55 — Release Approval

Release approval must be distinct from technical readiness.

A technically ready product may still be unsuitable for release because of unresolved customer, safety, legal, security, operational or governance concerns.

### Clause 56 — Product Change Management

Material product changes must undergo appropriate assessment, review, approval, verification and communication.

### Clause 57 — Product Version Integrity

Veyra must maintain appropriate version identification and change records.

Approvals and assurance evidence must identify the version or configuration to which they apply.

### Clause 58 — Product Retirement

Product retirement must address applicable customer commitments, notification, migration, data retrieval, continuity, dependencies, support arrangements and secure disposal.

### Clause 59 — Continued Fitness for Purpose

Products must be periodically reassessed to determine whether they remain appropriate for their intended purpose, users, operating environment and risk profile.

---

## PART VI — SERVICE DELIVERY AND OPERATIONS

### Clause 60 — Service Definition

Every service must have a documented definition identifying its scope, delivery method, intended users, responsibilities and operating conditions.

### Clause 61 — Service Delivery Authority

Service delivery must be performed by authorised people, systems or providers acting within defined responsibilities and permissions.

### Clause 62 — Service Readiness

A service must not enter production operation until operational readiness has been appropriately assessed.

### Clause 63 — Service Continuity

Veyra must establish continuity arrangements proportionate to the importance and consequences of service interruption.

### Clause 64 — Service Reliability

Services must have defined reliability expectations appropriate to their purpose and risk classification.

### Clause 65 — Availability Commitments

Where availability is a material service commitment, its measurement, calculation, exclusions and reporting method must be documented.

### Clause 66 — Service-Level Commitments

Service-level agreements and similar commitments must have:

1. Clearly defined measures.
2. A specified measurement period.
3. Defined responsibilities and exclusions.
4. Appropriate monitoring and reporting.
5. An authorised owner.
6. Defined escalation and remediation arrangements.

### Clause 67 — Service Performance

Service performance must be measured against declared expectations and actual service behaviour.

### Clause 68 — Service Monitoring

Services must be monitored according to their criticality, risk, operating environment and customer commitments.

### Clause 69 — Service Incidents

Incidents must be identified, classified, recorded, escalated and addressed through controlled incident-management arrangements.

### Clause 70 — Customer Impact Assessment

Significant incidents must include assessment of actual or potential customer, user and affected-individual impact.

### Clause 71 — Service Recovery

Recovery must restore service integrity and appropriate operating safeguards, not merely technical availability.

### Clause 72 — Service Changes

Changes must be assessed and controlled according to their potential impact on customers, continuity, security, privacy and contractual commitments.

### Clause 73 — Service Suspension

Veyra must retain the authority to suspend or restrict a service when necessary to protect customers, users, affected individuals, systems or the organisation.

### Clause 74 — Service Termination

Service termination must consider applicable customer commitments, continuity obligations, data handling, transition arrangements and appropriate notice.

### Clause 75 — Service Degradation Disclosure

Material degradation must not be concealed where it affects customer commitments, safety, service integrity or reasonable reliance.

---

## PART VII — TRANSPARENCY AND CUSTOMER COMMUNICATIONS

### Clause 76 — Accurate Product Information

Product descriptions, documentation, demonstrations, advertisements and representations must be accurate and must not create materially misleading impressions.

### Clause 77 — Capability Disclosure

Customers must receive appropriate information about relevant capabilities, limitations, dependencies and operating conditions.

### Clause 78 — Risk and Limitation Disclosure

Material risks, limitations, exclusions and conditions of use must be communicated in a manner appropriate to the product, service and intended audience.

### Clause 79 — AI and Automation Disclosure

Where AI or automation materially affects a customer's experience, decisions or outcomes, Veyra must provide appropriate transparency about its involvement.

### Clause 80 — Human Oversight Disclosure

Where human oversight is an important safeguard, Veyra must communicate its actual nature and limitations accurately.

### Clause 81 — No False Guarantees

Veyra must not promise absolute safety, uninterrupted availability, perfect accuracy, complete security or guaranteed outcomes without legitimate and demonstrable grounds.

### Clause 82 — Evidence-Backed Claims

Material claims concerning security, performance, accuracy, reliability, safety and availability must have appropriate supporting evidence and defined measurement methods.

### Clause 83 — Customer Notifications

Customers must receive appropriate notifications about material changes, significant incidents, interruptions, changes to terms and other matters that materially affect their use of a product or service.

### Clause 84 — Communication Integrity

Customer communications must be clear, timely, accurate, accessible and appropriately documented.

### Clause 85 — Marketing Integrity

Veyra must not use fabricated endorsements, misleading comparisons, deceptive urgency, manipulated performance claims or dishonest marketing practices.

### Clause 86 — Demonstration Integrity

Demonstrations must distinguish ordinary production behaviour from simulations, manual intervention, exceptional conditions and controlled demonstrations.

### Clause 87 — Material Changes in Product Behaviour

Veyra must communicate significant changes to product behaviour, capabilities, limitations or operating conditions where those changes materially affect customer expectations or reliance.

## PART VIII — CONSENT, DATA RIGHTS AND PRIVACY

### Clause 88 — Customer Data Responsibility

Veyra must treat customer and user information as a responsibility requiring appropriate governance, protection and accountability.

### Clause 89 — Data Rights and Custody Distinction

Possession, storage, technical control or processing of information must not be interpreted as unrestricted ownership or permission to use that information.

Veyra must distinguish applicable rights, custody, control, processing authority and contractual responsibilities.

### Clause 90 — Purpose Limitation

Information must only be collected, accessed, processed and used for legitimate and appropriately established purposes.

### Clause 91 — Data Minimisation

Veyra must avoid collecting or retaining information unnecessary for legitimate product or service purposes.

### Clause 92 — Consent Integrity

Where consent is the required basis for an activity, it must be meaningful, appropriately informed, specific to the relevant purpose and obtained through a valid process.

### Clause 93 — Separate Permissions

Consent to use a product or service must not automatically constitute consent to unrelated data processing, third-party disclosure, AI training, marketing or other distinct activities.

### Clause 94 — Consent Withdrawal

Where consent is relied upon, Veyra must provide an appropriate means of withdrawing it and must address the consequences of withdrawal in accordance with applicable obligations.

### Clause 95 — Consent Records

Where necessary, Veyra must maintain records sufficient to establish the scope, timing, purpose and status of consent.

### Clause 96 — Authority Is Not Consent

A person's technical access, account status or ability to issue an instruction must not automatically be interpreted as having authority to consent on behalf of another individual.

### Clause 97 — Data Protection

Customer information must be protected through appropriate technical, organisational and operational safeguards.

### Clause 98 — Access Control

Access to customer information must be authorised, justified, attributable and limited to the extent necessary.

### Clause 99 — Data Rights Mechanisms

Veyra must establish appropriate mechanisms for access, correction, retrieval, export, deletion and other applicable data rights.

### Clause 100 — Retention and Disposal

Information must not be retained indefinitely without a legitimate basis. Disposal must be appropriately controlled and documented where necessary.

### Clause 101 — AI Development and Customer Data

Customer information must not be used to train, improve, evaluate or otherwise develop AI systems unless the use is appropriately authorised and consistent with applicable law, contractual obligations and privacy requirements.

### Clause 102 — Customer Data Isolation

Products serving multiple customers or organisations must implement appropriate controls against unauthorised cross-customer access, disclosure or interference.

### Clause 103 — Data Breaches and Exposure

Suspected or confirmed material exposure, loss, unauthorised access or misuse of customer information must be assessed and escalated under applicable incident-management and notification requirements.

### Clause 104 — Data Portability and Exit

Where appropriate and legally permissible, customers must have reasonable mechanisms to retrieve their information in usable formats and transition away from Veyra services.

### Clause 105 — Privacy Governance Alignment

Customer data and privacy practices must comply with the applicable security, trust and privacy requirements of the Veyra Constitution.

## PART IX — CUSTOMER SUPPORT, COMPLAINTS AND REDRESS

### Clause 106 — Customer Support

Veyra must establish support arrangements appropriate to the nature, complexity, criticality and commitments of its products and services.

### Clause 107 — Support Accountability

Support activities must have identifiable ownership, defined responsibilities and appropriate escalation authority.

### Clause 108 — Complaint Access

Customers and users must have accessible means of submitting complaints and concerns.

### Clause 109 — Complaint Integrity

Complaints must be recorded, assessed fairly and handled without suppression, retaliation or unjustified obstruction.

### Clause 110 — Complaint Escalation

Serious complaints, recurring failures, safety concerns, suspected misconduct and material service failures must be escalated appropriately.

### Clause 111 — Independent Review

Where a complaint concerns a decision or action by the person responsible for resolving it, Veyra must provide an appropriate alternative review authority where reasonably necessary.

### Clause 112 — Customer Redress

Where Veyra is responsible for a substantiated failure, it must assess appropriate corrective action or redress consistent with applicable obligations.

### Clause 113 — Dispute Management

Veyra must establish fair mechanisms for resolving customer disputes and must not mislead customers about their rights or available remedies.

### Clause 114 — Vulnerable Customers

Where customers or users may be vulnerable, Veyra must consider additional safeguards appropriate to their circumstances.

### Clause 115 — Children and Other Protected Groups

Where a product or service is intended for, or reasonably likely to be used by, children or other groups requiring additional protection, Veyra must establish appropriate safeguards, access controls and age- or context-appropriate design.

### Clause 116 — Feedback and Improvement

Support requests, complaints and customer feedback must contribute to product and service improvement where relevant.

## PART X — COMMERCIAL, CONTRACTUAL AND THIRD-PARTY GOVERNANCE

### Clause 117 — Commercial Integrity

Commercial decisions must remain consistent with constitutional obligations.

Revenue, profitability and growth must not justify deception, exploitation or unacceptable risk.

### Clause 118 — Transparent Commercial Terms

Products and services must have appropriately documented commercial terms, including relevant pricing, charges, renewals, limitations and cancellation arrangements.

### Clause 119 — No Hidden Material Conditions

Veyra must not intentionally conceal material conditions that would reasonably affect a customer's decision to purchase, subscribe to or use a product or service.

### Clause 120 — Fair Subscription and Renewal Practices

Recurring charges, automatic renewals, cancellation requirements and material changes to subscription terms must be disclosed clearly and handled fairly.

### Clause 121 — Contractual Authority

Contracts, service agreements and customer commitments must be approved by appropriately authorised persons or authorities.

### Clause 122 — Contractual Consistency

Commercial agreements must not authorise conduct prohibited by the Veyra Constitution.

### Clause 123 — Customer Commitment Records

Material commitments made to customers must be documented, assigned to accountable owners and monitored for fulfilment.

### Clause 124 — Third-Party Providers

Third parties materially contributing to Veyra products or services must undergo appropriate evaluation, authorisation, contractual control and ongoing oversight.

### Clause 125 — Third-Party Accountability

The use of an external provider does not eliminate Veyra's responsibility for its own decisions, representations and obligations.

### Clause 126 — Subcontracting and Downstream Providers

Where providers rely on subcontractors or downstream services, Veyra must establish appropriate visibility, accountability and safeguards proportionate to the risks involved.

### Clause 127 — Provider Changes

Material changes to suppliers, providers, platforms or dependencies must be assessed for their effects on customers, quality, security, continuity and contractual commitments.

### Clause 128 — Customer-Facing Partnerships

Partnerships involving customer access, data, product delivery or service operation must have clearly defined authority, responsibilities and accountability.

### Clause 129 — Conflicts of Interest

Actual, potential or perceived conflicts of interest affecting customer treatment, product decisions, provider selection or service delivery must be identified and appropriately managed.

## PART XI — AI-POWERED PRODUCTS AND AUTONOMOUS SERVICES

### Clause 130 — AI Product Accountability

Every AI-powered product or service must have an identifiable accountable owner.

AI use does not remove Veyra's responsibility.

### Clause 131 — Defined AI Operating Boundaries

AI capabilities must operate within documented functional, data, permission and autonomy boundaries.

### Clause 132 — Capability Does Not Confer Authority

An AI system must not acquire authority merely because it is technically capable of performing an action.

### Clause 133 — Explicit Delegation

Authority delegated to an AI system must be defined by an authorised person and must specify the permitted actions, scope, limits and applicable conditions.

### Clause 134 — Human Authority

Decisions and actions requiring human authority must remain subject to the appropriate human decision-maker.

### Clause 135 — Permission Separation

AI permissions must distinguish, as appropriate, between reading information, preparing actions, recommending actions, executing reversible actions and executing consequential or irreversible actions.

### Clause 136 — User Control and Revocation

Users must have appropriate means of reviewing, changing and revoking delegated AI permissions, subject to legitimate operational and legal requirements.

### Clause 137 — Consequential Automated Decisions

AI-powered products must identify decisions that may materially affect a person's rights, opportunities, finances, safety, access or other significant interests.

Such decisions require heightened assessment, safeguards, accountability and appropriate human review mechanisms.

### Clause 138 — Contestability and Appeal

Where an AI-generated decision materially affects an individual, Veyra must provide an appropriate mechanism to challenge or request review of that decision where required by the nature of the decision or applicable obligations.

### Clause 139 — AI Behaviour Transparency

Customers and users must receive appropriate information about material AI behaviour, relevant limitations and foreseeable reliance risks.

### Clause 140 — Human Intervention

AI-powered services must provide appropriate human intervention, escalation or review where required by their risk, function or operating commitments.

### Clause 141 — AI Failure and Misbehaviour

Materially incorrect outputs, unexpected actions, boundary violations and harmful AI behaviour must be treated as product or service incidents where appropriate.

### Clause 142 — AI Autonomy Changes

Material increases in autonomy, permissions, external access or decision-making authority require formal assessment and approval.

### Clause 143 — No Concealed Material Automation

Veyra must not intentionally conceal material automated activity where customers or affected individuals reasonably need to understand that automation is involved.

### Clause 144 — AI Monitoring and Reassessment

AI products and services must be monitored and reassessed for changes in behaviour, performance, reliability, safety and customer impact.

### Clause 145 — AI Restriction and Withdrawal

Veyra must retain the authority to restrict, suspend, roll back or withdraw AI capabilities when continued operation presents unacceptable risks.

### Clause 146 — AION Governance

AION must comply with this document as a Veyra product.

Its personal intelligence capabilities, memory, integrations, delegated authority, external actions, user permissions and data practices must be governed by explicit product-level requirements consistent with this Constitution.

AION must not receive exemptions from Veyra's customer, user, safety, privacy or accountability obligations merely because it is a foundational or strategically important product.

## PART XII — CUSTOMER RISK, SAFETY AND ACCOUNTABILITY

### Clause 147 — Customer Impact Risk Assessment

Products and services must undergo appropriate assessment of potential customer, user, affected-individual and public impact before release and following material changes.

### Clause 148 — Risk-Based Safeguards

Safeguards must be proportionate to the severity, likelihood, exposure and reversibility of potential harm.

### Clause 149 — Foreseeable Misuse

Governance must consider reasonably foreseeable misuse, unintended use, abuse, manipulation and exploitation.

### Clause 150 — Misuse Prevention

Veyra must establish appropriate controls to prevent or reduce foreseeable harmful uses of its products and services.

### Clause 151 — Product Safety Governance

Products and services must be assessed for safety risks arising from their intended purpose, design, operation, failure modes and foreseeable use.

### Clause 152 — Safety-Critical Use

Products intended for, or reasonably capable of influencing, safety-critical activities require heightened governance, assurance and authorisation.

### Clause 153 — Reversibility and Consequence

Where a product or service can perform actions with significant or irreversible consequences, its design and governance must include safeguards appropriate to those consequences.

### Clause 154 — Customer Impact Monitoring

Veyra must establish mechanisms to identify material negative customer outcomes and emerging patterns of harm.

### Clause 155 — Material Harm Escalation

Material harm, credible threats to customer safety, significant privacy violations and serious misuse must be escalated without unreasonable delay.

### Clause 156 — Corrective Action

Identified customer-impacting failures must result in proportionate corrective action, including product changes, notifications, service restriction or withdrawal where necessary.

### Clause 157 — Incident Communication

Where incidents materially affect customers or their interests, Veyra must communicate relevant information in accordance with applicable obligations and the need to protect investigations and affected parties.

### Clause 158 — Non-Retaliation

No individual must be penalised for raising a good-faith concern about customer safety, product integrity, service quality or governance failures.

### Clause 159 — Accountability Records

Veyra must maintain appropriate records of significant product decisions, service approvals, customer-impacting incidents, risk assessments, material changes and corrective actions.

---

## PART XIII — GOVERNANCE, OVERSIGHT AND PERFORMANCE

### Clause 160 — Governance Responsibilities

Veyra must establish clear allocations of authority and responsibility for:

1. Product strategy and approval.
2. Product ownership.
3. Service ownership.
4. Customer relationship governance.
5. Customer rights and data governance.
6. Customer support and complaint management.
7. Risk assessment and acceptance.
8. Commercial and contractual approval.
9. Third-party oversight.
10. Product and service retirement.

### Clause 161 — Separation of Responsibilities

Where appropriate to the risk and scale of an activity, product ownership, engineering assurance, operational approval and independent oversight must be sufficiently separated to prevent unchecked self-approval.

### Clause 162 — Decision Documentation

Material product, service and customer-governance decisions must record their rationale, authority, relevant evidence, known limitations and identified risks.

### Clause 163 — Customer Governance Audits

Veyra must retain the ability to examine whether its customer, product and service governance obligations are being followed.

### Clause 164 — Performance and Outcome Review

Product and service performance must be evaluated against intended customer outcomes, not solely revenue, usage, engagement or technical output.

### Clause 165 — Customer Outcome Indicators

Veyra must establish appropriate indicators for customer satisfaction, service quality, reliability, complaint patterns, customer-impacting incidents and other relevant outcomes.

### Clause 166 — Metrics Integrity

Customer and product metrics must be accurate, appropriately contextualised and must not be manipulated to conceal poor performance or misleadingly demonstrate success.

### Clause 167 — Continuous Improvement

Veyra must use customer experience, incidents, complaints, operational evidence and product performance to improve its products and services.

### Clause 168 — Periodic Governance Review

Customer, product and service governance arrangements must be reviewed periodically and when material changes, incidents or emerging risks justify reassessment.

It must also be reconciled with 00.15 and subsequent constitutional documents when developed and approved.

### Clause 170 — Lower-Level Governance

All policies, procedures, product specifications, service definitions, customer agreements and operational rules must comply with this document.

### Clause 171 — No Contradictory Subordinate Rules

No subordinate policy, contract, automated workflow or operational instruction may create an exception to a constitutional requirement unless the Constitution expressly permits that exception.

### Clause 172 — Exceptions

Exceptions to subordinate requirements must be authorised, justified, documented, limited in scope and subject to review.

No exception may override applicable law, constitutional prohibitions, mandatory human authority or non-waivable security and trust obligations.

### Clause 173 — No Commercial or Operational Override

No commercial target, executive preference, customer demand, partnership agreement or operational convenience may justify violation of this document.

### Clause 174 — Right to Restrict or Withdraw

Veyra retains the authority to restrict, suspend, discontinue or withdraw a product or service when necessary to protect customers, users, affected individuals, the organisation or the public.

### Clause 175 — Enforcement and Corrective Governance

Identified violations must be assessed and addressed through appropriate corrective, disciplinary, operational or governance processes consistent with applicable authority and due process.

### Clause 176 — Constitutional Amendment

Amendments to this document must follow the amendment and document governance procedures established by 00.10.

### Clause 177 — Foundational Commitment

Veyra exists to create products and services that deserve the confidence placed in them.

That confidence must be earned through genuine value, responsible engineering, honest communication, reliable operation, respect for customers and users, and accountability for consequences.

No product, service, commercial success or technical achievement can justify abandoning these obligations.
