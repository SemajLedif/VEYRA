# VEYRA — Baseline Requirements & Constraints

**Document ID:** VEYRA-00.7  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Canonical Working Draft  
**Parent:** Phase 0 — Foundation / Constitution  
**Depends On:** VEYRA-00.1 Project Identity, VEYRA-00.2 Vision & Mission, VEYRA-00.3 Scope & Boundaries, VEYRA-00.4 Core Principles, VEYRA-00.5 Definitions & Terminology, VEYRA-00.6 Veyra / AION Relationship

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the baseline requirements and constraints that govern the development of Veyra.

These requirements define what Veyra must fundamentally be capable of becoming and the conditions within which the system must be designed.

They are not intended to replace the detailed requirements engineering performed later in the Master Design Order.

Instead, they establish the foundational boundary conditions from which later requirements are derived.

---

## 2. General Baseline Requirement

Veyra shall be designed as a general-purpose software-engineering intelligence capable of supporting software projects across a broad range of technologies, architectures, programming languages, frameworks, infrastructure environments, and project types.

Its design must not depend fundamentally on one specific software project.

---

## 3. Software Lifecycle Coverage

Veyra shall ultimately be capable of supporting the major stages of software engineering, including where applicable:

- problem understanding;
- research;
- requirements analysis;
- planning;
- architecture;
- system design;
- implementation;
- testing;
- verification;
- debugging;
- security analysis;
- deployment;
- operations;
- maintenance;
- evolution.

The final division of these capabilities into subsystems shall be established through later research and architecture.

---

## 4. Project-Agnostic Capability

Veyra shall be capable of being applied to projects other than AION.

The system should be able to accommodate projects with substantially different:

- purposes;
- technology stacks;
- architectures;
- scales;
- requirements;
- deployment environments;
- development methodologies.

AION-specific assumptions must not become hidden dependencies of Veyra's core design.

---

## 5. Technology-Stack Flexibility

Veyra should support heterogeneous technology environments.

The system must not be constitutionally bound to:

- one programming language;
- one framework;
- one database;
- one cloud provider;
- one operating system;
- one development environment;
- one version-control platform;
- one AI model;
- one model provider.

Specific technology support will be established through research and implementation.

---

## 6. Intelligence Resource Flexibility

Veyra shall be designed to incorporate multiple forms of engineering intelligence where useful.

Potential resources include:

- frontier AI models;
- open-weight models;
- coding models;
- reasoning models;
- local models;
- deterministic software;
- specialized services;
- human expertise.

No single intelligence resource should be assumed to be sufficient for every engineering task.

---

## 7. Model Independence

The architecture should minimize unnecessary dependence on a single AI model or provider.

Where practical, models should be replaceable or interchangeable without requiring fundamental reconstruction of Veyra.

Model selection should remain an architectural and operational decision informed by:

- capability;
- reliability;
- cost;
- latency;
- availability;
- licensing;
- security;
- privacy;
- task requirements.

---

## 8. Tool Integration

Veyra shall ultimately be capable of interacting with relevant software-engineering tools.

Potential tool categories include:

- source control;
- repositories;
- terminals;
- code execution;
- testing;
- package managers;
- build systems;
- documentation;
- research systems;
- deployment systems;
- cloud infrastructure;
- observability systems.

Tool integration must remain governed by authorization and security boundaries.

---

## 9. Execution Capability

Veyra must eventually be capable of causing software-engineering actions to occur rather than being limited to generating recommendations.

Depending on authorization, this may include:

- creating files;
- modifying code;
- running tests;
- executing programs;
- creating branches;
- committing changes;
- opening or updating pull requests;
- deploying software;
- inspecting runtime behavior;
- performing maintenance operations.

Execution authority shall be constrained by security and governance requirements.

---

## 10. Verification Requirement

Veyra shall treat verification as an essential part of engineering.

Generated or modified software should not be considered complete solely because an intelligence resource reports that the work is finished.

The system should be capable of applying appropriate verification mechanisms based on:

- requirements;
- risk;
- project characteristics;
- software architecture;
- deployment context.

---

## 11. Closed-Loop Operation

Veyra should support feedback between engineering stages.

The system should be capable of responding to outcomes such as:

- failed tests;
- rejected changes;
- runtime errors;
- security findings;
- deployment failures;
- performance problems;
- requirement changes.

These outcomes should be capable of driving further investigation, modification, and verification.

---

## 12. Context Requirement

Veyra must be capable of maintaining and supplying relevant context to engineering processes.

Context management must account for:

- project size;
- task complexity;
- model limitations;
- stale information;
- conflicting information;
- irrelevant information;
- context-window limitations.

The architecture should avoid assuming that all project information can or should be supplied to every operation.

---

## 13. Memory Requirement

Veyra shall require mechanisms for retaining useful project information across appropriate periods of work.

The eventual system should be capable of preserving relevant information such as:

- decisions;
- project knowledge;
- requirements;
- history;
- task state;
- engineering discoveries;
- prior failures;
- verification results.

The exact memory architecture is deliberately deferred to later research and architecture.

---

## 14. State Awareness

Veyra must be capable of determining the relevant state of the software project before performing consequential actions.

Where appropriate, this includes understanding:

- current source state;
- repository state;
- requirements state;
- task state;
- test state;
- deployment state;
- operational state.

The system should avoid acting solely from assumptions about the current state.

---

## 15. Coordination Requirement

Veyra shall ultimately support coordination among multiple engineering capabilities.

This may involve:

- task decomposition;
- assignment;
- sequencing;
- dependency management;
- communication;
- result integration;
- conflict resolution;
- escalation;
- verification.

The exact agent and orchestration model remains subject to research.

---

## 16. Dynamic Specialization

Veyra should be capable of composing specialized capabilities according to the requirements of a particular engineering problem.

The system should not require every project to use the same fixed collection of agents or workflows.

Specialization should be created where it provides measurable engineering value.

---

## 17. Human Governance Requirement

Veyra shall provide mechanisms through which authorized humans can:

- establish objectives;
- establish constraints;
- grant authority;
- review important actions;
- intervene;
- stop execution;
- revoke permissions;
- modify governance.

The exact human-in-the-loop and human-on-the-loop mechanisms remain subject to later architecture.

---

## 18. Permission Requirement

Veyra shall operate under explicit permission boundaries.

Capabilities that can affect external systems should not automatically receive unrestricted authority.

Permissions should be appropriately:

- scoped;
- controlled;
- auditable;
- revocable.

---

## 19. Security Requirement

Security shall be treated as a foundational requirement rather than a later feature.

The system must account for threats involving:

- malicious inputs;
- compromised dependencies;
- untrusted code;
- malicious tools;
- credential exposure;
- unauthorized actions;
- cross-project access;
- compromised agents;
- model manipulation;
- infrastructure compromise.

Detailed security architecture will be developed later.

---

## 20. Isolation Requirement

Veyra should provide appropriate isolation between projects, users, execution environments, tools, and other security domains.

The degree of isolation shall depend on:

- trust;
- permissions;
- data sensitivity;
- execution risk;
- deployment model.

---

## 21. Auditability Requirement

Consequential engineering actions should be sufficiently observable and traceable to support investigation and governance.

Where appropriate, the system should preserve information about:

- who or what initiated an action;
- what action occurred;
- when it occurred;
- what resources were used;
- what result occurred;
- what authorization applied.

---

## 22. Failure Handling Requirement

Veyra must be designed to expect failure.

The system should support appropriate responses to:

- model failures;
- reasoning errors;
- tool failures;
- execution failures;
- test failures;
- infrastructure failures;
- coordination failures;
- security incidents.

Failure should not automatically result in uncontrolled continuation.

---

## 23. Recovery Requirement

Where practical, Veyra should support recovery mechanisms such as:

- retry;
- rollback;
- repair;
- isolation;
- alternative execution;
- escalation.

The appropriate recovery strategy should depend on the type and severity of failure.

---

## 24. Reversibility Requirement

Consequential operations should be reversible where technically and economically practical.

Where an operation cannot reasonably be reversed, the system should apply appropriate additional controls before execution.

---

## 25. Observability Requirement

Veyra must eventually provide sufficient observability to understand the behavior and state of its major operations.

Observability should extend, where appropriate, to:

- agents;
- workers;
- orchestration;
- tool calls;
- execution environments;
- verification;
- infrastructure;
- deployed software.

---

## 26. Resource Awareness

Veyra must operate within finite computational and economic resources.

The architecture should account for:

- compute;
- memory;
- storage;
- bandwidth;
- model inference;
- API limits;
- execution time;
- infrastructure;
- operational cost.

---

## 27. Development Cost Constraint

During initial development, Veyra should prioritize free and open-source technologies and free tiers wherever they provide sufficient capability.

This constraint exists to make substantial development possible with limited initial resources.

It does not prohibit later adoption of paid technologies where their value justifies the cost.

---

## 28. Commercial Viability Constraint

The eventual Veyra platform should be capable of operating as a commercially usable service.

The architecture should therefore remain capable of supporting future concerns such as:

- multiple users;
- project isolation;
- usage management;
- reliability;
- scalability;
- service operations;
- billing;
- security;
- data governance.

These concerns should not be allowed to prematurely dictate detailed architecture before the appropriate research phase.

---

## 29. Extensibility Requirement

Veyra should be designed so that new capabilities can be introduced without unnecessary reconstruction of the entire system.

Potential future extensions may include:

- new models;
- new tools;
- new engineering workflows;
- new execution environments;
- new verification mechanisms;
- new infrastructure;
- new interfaces.

---

## 30. Replaceability Requirement

Major external dependencies should be replaceable where practical.

This applies particularly to:

- model providers;
- AI models;
- tools;
- infrastructure;
- execution systems;
- storage systems.

Replacement may not always be economically or technically practical; such exceptions should be explicit.

---

## 31. Documentation Requirement

Important engineering knowledge must be represented in durable project artifacts.

Documentation should support:

- continuity;
- traceability;
- decision-making;
- architecture;
- implementation;
- operations;
- future evolution.

Documentation volume must remain proportional to actual engineering need.

---

## 32. Traceability Requirement

Important relationships should be traceable where practical.

Examples include:

```text
Objective
   ↓
Requirement
   ↓
Design Decision
   ↓
Implementation
   ↓
Verification
   ↓
Result
```

The precise traceability system will be established in later phases.

---

## 33. Evidence Requirement

Important engineering decisions should be supported by appropriate evidence.

Where evidence is insufficient, the project should identify the decision as provisional or uncertain rather than presenting it as established fact.

---

## 34. Research Requirement

Major unknowns affecting Veyra's architecture must be investigated before irreversible architectural commitments are made.

Research should be directed toward decisions and engineering outcomes.

---

## 35. Architecture Constraint

Implementation must not prematurely determine foundational architecture.

The project should establish sufficient:

- research;
- requirements;
- constraints;
- architectural reasoning

before committing to major implementation choices.

---

## 36. No Premature Lock-In

Veyra shall avoid unnecessary early commitment to:

- specific AI providers;
- specific agent frameworks;
- specific orchestration frameworks;
- specific databases;
- specific cloud platforms;
- specific execution technologies.

A technology may be adopted provisionally for experimentation without becoming constitutionally mandatory.

---

## 37. No Artificial Complexity

The system shall not become more complex merely to appear sophisticated.

Every significant architectural mechanism should have a defensible purpose.

This includes:

- agents;
- services;
- workflows;
- databases;
- abstractions;
- infrastructure;
- communication mechanisms.

---

## 38. No Artificial Simplicity

Conversely, Veyra shall not reject necessary complexity merely because simplicity is aesthetically preferable.

If reliable general-purpose software engineering requires:

- sophisticated orchestration;
- specialized intelligence;
- persistent state;
- deep verification;
- isolation;
- complex infrastructure;

the architecture should be allowed to provide it.

---

## 39. Generalization Constraint

Veyra should generalize where generalization is supported by evidence and repeated engineering need.

It should avoid both:

1. over-specialization around one project; and
2. premature abstractions built without demonstrated need.

---

## 40. Self-Improvement Constraint

Future self-improvement capabilities must operate within explicit safety and governance boundaries.

Veyra must not be assumed to have unrestricted authority to modify:

- its own core;
- its security boundaries;
- its permissions;
- its infrastructure;
- its governance mechanisms.

---

## 41. Self-Engineering Constraint

Veyra may eventually contribute to the development of Veyra itself.

However, the ability to modify itself does not remove the need for:

- testing;
- review;
- verification;
- governance;
- rollback;
- human authority where appropriate.

---

## 42. AION Independence Constraint

Veyra shall remain independently meaningful from AION.

AION may be a major use case and validation target for Veyra, but Veyra must remain capable of serving unrelated software projects.

---

## 43. Development Environment Constraint

The initial development environment may be constrained by available hardware, infrastructure, and budget.

The architecture should therefore permit progressive scaling from constrained development environments toward substantially larger production infrastructure.

Early limitations must not automatically become permanent architectural limitations.

---

## 44. Progressive Capability

Veyra does not need to possess its ultimate capabilities on the first implementation.

Development should permit progressive growth from:

```text
Foundational system
        ↓
Initial engineering capabilities
        ↓
Integrated engineering workflows
        ↓
Multi-capability orchestration
        ↓
Advanced autonomous engineering
        ↓
Large-scale engineering intelligence
```

Each stage should be validated before relying on capabilities that have not yet been demonstrated.

---

## 45. Constraint Hierarchy

When multiple constraints conflict, they should be considered according to their significance.

A useful conceptual hierarchy is:

```text
Human safety and authority
        ↓
Security and isolation
        ↓
Correctness and verification
        ↓
Core mission
        ↓
Reliability and recoverability
        ↓
Maintainability and extensibility
        ↓
Cost and efficiency
        ↓
Convenience
```

This hierarchy is a governing heuristic, not a substitute for explicit decision-making.

---

## 46. Requirement Evolution

These baseline requirements may be refined as the project progresses.

However, material changes must be:

- documented;
- justified;
- traceable;
- evaluated for downstream impact.

Later requirements may be more specific than this document but should not silently contradict foundational constraints.

---

## 47. Relationship to Later Requirements

This document establishes **baseline requirements**, not the complete Veyra requirements specification.

Later phases will determine:

- functional requirements;
- non-functional requirements;
- system requirements;
- subsystem requirements;
- interfaces;
- performance requirements;
- security requirements;
- operational requirements;
- commercial requirements.

Those requirements should trace back to the foundational conditions established here where appropriate.

---

## 48. Status

This document establishes the baseline requirements and constraints for the Veyra project.

It is intentionally broad enough to permit research and architectural discovery while establishing boundaries strong enough to prevent arbitrary design drift.

Future phases may refine, extend, or supersede individual requirements through the established governance process.

---

## 49. Baseline Summary

At the constitutional level, Veyra must be:

- general-purpose within software engineering;
- lifecycle-oriented;
- technology-flexible;
- model-flexible;
- tool-capable;
- execution-capable;
- verification-oriented;
- context-aware;
- memory-enabled;
- coordination-capable;
- human-governed;
- security-first;
- permission-controlled;
- observable;
- auditable;
- failure-aware;
- recoverable;
- extensible;
- replaceable where practical;
- cost-aware;
- commercially viable;
- capable of progressive evolution.

The detailed mechanism by which these requirements are achieved belongs to later research, requirements engineering, and architecture.
