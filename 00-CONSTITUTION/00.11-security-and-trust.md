# 00.11 — Security and Trust

**Document ID:** 00.11  
**Document Class:** Constitutional  
**Status:** Approved  
**Parent Directory:** `00-CONSTITUTION/`

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the foundational security principles, trust requirements, protective boundaries and governance obligations that shall guide the design, development, operation and evolution of Veyra.

Security and trust are fundamental properties of Veyra. They must be considered throughout its lifecycle rather than introduced as isolated features or late-stage engineering activities.

Veyra must protect its systems, information, users, development environment, external integrations, artificial intelligence capabilities and operational functions against unauthorized access, misuse, compromise, manipulation, disruption and unintended consequences.

This document establishes the constitutional foundation upon which Veyra's detailed security architecture, technical controls, operational procedures and assurance mechanisms shall be developed.

## 2. Foundational Security Principle

Security must be treated as an architectural, operational and governance requirement.

Veyra shall adopt a security-conscious engineering philosophy in which:

- Security is considered from the beginning of system design.
- Trust must be established through evidence and verification.
- Access must be limited to legitimate and necessary purposes.
- Sensitive information must receive appropriate protection.
- Authority must be explicitly defined and enforced.
- Security controls must be proportionate to risk.
- Failures and vulnerabilities must be acknowledged and addressed.
- Security decisions must remain traceable and reviewable.
- Protective mechanisms must not depend solely on the good intentions of users, agents or external systems.
- Security must remain effective even when individual components or trust assumptions fail.

No performance objective, development convenience or automation requirement shall automatically justify weakening essential security protections.

## 3. Security by Design and by Default

Veyra shall incorporate security into its architecture, engineering processes and operational practices.

Security by design requires that potential threats, vulnerabilities, misuse cases and failure conditions be considered before relevant systems are implemented.

Security by default requires that components begin in their most restrictive reasonable configuration and that additional permissions or capabilities be enabled only when justified.

Veyra shall:

- Minimize exposed functionality.
- Reduce unnecessary dependencies.
- Restrict privileged operations.
- Separate sensitive responsibilities.
- Validate inputs and outputs.
- Protect internal and external communication.
- Establish secure defaults.
- Avoid unnecessary exposure of services and information.
- Provide mechanisms for detecting and containing failures.
- Prefer secure and verifiable architectural choices over convenience-driven alternatives.

Security must not depend on undocumented assumptions or manual precautions alone.

## 4. Threat Modelling and Risk Assessment

Veyra shall use systematic threat modelling and security risk assessment appropriate to the complexity, exposure and potential impact of its components.

Threat assessment should consider:

- Unauthorized access.
- Privilege escalation.
- Credential theft.
- Malicious or compromised dependencies.
- Supply-chain attacks.
- Data leakage.
- Prompt injection and instruction manipulation.
- Malicious repositories and files.
- Compromised external services.
- Unsafe code execution.
- Network-based attacks.
- Denial of service.
- Destructive or unauthorized modifications.
- Insider threats and misuse of legitimate access.
- Social engineering.
- Abuse of automated capabilities.
- Model compromise and malicious model behavior.
- Infrastructure and configuration compromise.
- Physical threats to relevant infrastructure.

Threat models must evolve as Veyra gains new capabilities, integrations, permissions and operational responsibilities.

Significant security risks must be documented, evaluated, prioritized and assigned appropriate mitigation strategies.

Unresolved material risks must not be concealed or treated as automatically acceptable.

## 5. Identity, Authentication and Authorization

Veyra shall distinguish between identity, authentication and authorization.

Identity establishes which human, service, component or agent is requesting access.

Authentication establishes whether the claimed identity can be sufficiently verified.

Authorization establishes which actions that authenticated identity is permitted to perform.

Access to protected resources must be based on explicit authorization rather than identity alone.

Where appropriate, Veyra should support:

- Strong authentication.
- Multi-factor authentication.
- Secure session management.
- Individual and service identities.
- Credential rotation.
- Revocation of compromised credentials.
- Role-based or attribute-based access control.
- Authentication of external integrations.
- Verification of privileged operations.
- Short-lived credentials.
- Authentication of machine-to-machine communication.

Authentication and authorization mechanisms must be designed to resist impersonation, replay, privilege escalation and unauthorized delegation.

## 6. Least Privilege and Separation of Duties

Veyra shall enforce the principle of least privilege.

Every human, agent, service, process and component must receive only the permissions necessary to perform its authorized responsibilities.

Privileges must be:

- Explicitly granted.
- Limited in scope.
- Limited in duration where practical.
- Revocable.
- Reviewable.
- Appropriate to the risk of the operation.

Veyra should separate sensitive responsibilities so that a single compromised component or identity cannot unnecessarily control an entire system.

Critical operations should require additional safeguards where appropriate.

Possession of a valid credential, tool connection or execution environment must not automatically confer unrestricted authority.

The ability to request, recommend or initiate an operation must be distinguished from the authority to approve and execute it.

## 7. Secrets and Credential Management

Secrets and credentials are protected security assets.

They include:

- Passwords.
- API keys.
- Access tokens.
- Private cryptographic keys.
- SSH keys.
- Certificates.
- Session credentials.
- Database credentials.
- Service-account secrets.
- Encryption keys.
- Signing keys.

Veyra shall establish secure processes for creating, storing, accessing, transmitting, rotating, revoking and retiring secrets.

Secrets must not be unnecessarily exposed through:

- Source code.
- Public repositories.
- Logs.
- Documentation.
- Error messages.
- Terminal output.
- Build artifacts.
- Untrusted external services.
- AI prompts or model context.

Sensitive credentials must be stored using appropriate secure mechanisms.

Veyra must not request, collect or retain credentials beyond legitimate operational requirements.

Suspected credential exposure must be treated as a security incident requiring appropriate containment, revocation and remediation.

## 8. Cryptographic Security and Key Governance

Veyra shall use cryptographic mechanisms appropriate to the sensitivity, purpose and risk of the information and operations being protected.

Cryptographic controls may support:

- Confidentiality.
- Integrity.
- Authentication.
- Digital signatures.
- Secure communication.
- Artifact verification.
- Identity verification.
- Protection of stored information.

Cryptographic algorithms and implementations must be selected according to recognized security standards and current security guidance.

Key management must address:

- Secure key generation.
- Key storage and protection.
- Key distribution.
- Access restrictions.
- Rotation.
- Revocation.
- Expiration.
- Recovery where appropriate.
- Retirement and destruction.

Private keys and other cryptographic secrets must receive appropriate protection against unauthorized disclosure and modification.

Veyra must not invent or casually implement its own cryptographic algorithms when established, appropriately reviewed mechanisms are available.

Cryptographic protections must be evaluated in the context of their complete implementation rather than assumed effective merely because encryption is present.

## 9. Data Protection, Privacy and Information Classification

Veyra shall protect information according to its sensitivity, purpose, ownership and applicable legal or contractual obligations.

Data protection must consider:

- Confidentiality.
- Integrity.
- Availability.
- Authenticity.
- Privacy.
- Retention.
- Access control.
- Secure deletion.
- Data provenance.
- Lawful and legitimate processing.

Veyra must distinguish between public information, internal project information, confidential information, sensitive operational information and secrets.

Information should be collected and retained only where justified.

Privacy considerations must be incorporated into relevant system designs, data-processing activities and external integrations.

Where applicable, Veyra must respect data-subject rights, consent requirements, purpose limitations and legal retention obligations.

Data must not be disclosed to external services or integrations without appropriate authorization and consideration of the relevant risks.

## 10. Data Lifecycle and Secure Information Handling

Veyra shall govern sensitive information throughout its lifecycle.

This lifecycle includes:

1. Collection or creation.
2. Classification.
3. Storage.
4. Access and processing.
5. Transmission.
6. Retention.
7. Archiving.
8. Deletion or destruction.

Each stage must apply appropriate security and privacy protections.

Veyra should minimize unnecessary copying, duplication and exposure of sensitive information.

Temporary files, cached information, exported records and generated artifacts must not become uncontrolled secondary repositories of confidential data.

Deletion procedures must consider relevant backups, replicas, logs and other retained copies.

Where complete deletion is technically or legally restricted, the limitation must be documented and the information protected appropriately.

## 11. Secure Software Development

Security must be integrated into Veyra's software development lifecycle.

Engineering activities should incorporate:

- Secure coding practices.
- Input validation.
- Output handling.
- Dependency assessment.
- Static analysis.
- Dynamic security testing where appropriate.
- Code review.
- Authentication and authorization testing.
- Secure configuration.
- Error handling.
- Security regression testing.
- Verification of security-sensitive changes.

Security defects must be treated as engineering defects and managed according to their severity and potential impact.

Security requirements must be traceable to implementation and verification evidence where appropriate.

No implementation should be considered secure merely because it compiles, passes ordinary functional tests or appears to behave correctly under normal conditions.

## 12. Supply-Chain and Dependency Security

Veyra shall recognize that external dependencies and development tools introduce security risks.

Third-party packages, libraries, frameworks, models, plugins, build tools, container images and external services must not be trusted automatically.

Dependency governance should consider:

- Source authenticity.
- Publisher identity.
- Version integrity.
- Known vulnerabilities.
- Maintenance status.
- Licensing.
- Dependency provenance.
- Transitive dependencies.
- Integrity verification.
- Availability of security updates.

Veyra should maintain visibility into the components used to construct and operate its systems.

Where appropriate, it should use dependency pinning, integrity verification, software bills of materials and controlled update procedures.

Unverified or suspicious dependencies must not be introduced into trusted environments without appropriate assessment and authorization.

## 13. Software Build, CI/CD and Artifact Integrity

Veyra shall protect the integrity of its software development, build, testing, packaging and deployment pipelines.

Build and deployment infrastructure must be treated as security-sensitive systems.

Controls should address:

- Authentication of pipeline identities.
- Restricted pipeline permissions.
- Protection of build secrets.
- Isolation of untrusted build jobs.
- Verification of source revisions.
- Integrity of build inputs.
- Dependency verification.
- Build artifact integrity.
- Deployment authorization.
- Protection of release credentials.
- Traceability between source code and released artifacts.

Where appropriate, Veyra should support signed artifacts, verified provenance, reproducible builds and controlled release procedures.

A successful build must not automatically establish that an artifact is trustworthy.

Deployment of sensitive or production systems must follow the relevant authorization and verification requirements.

## 14. Network and External-System Security

Veyra must treat external systems and network connections as potential security boundaries.

Communication with external services must be governed by explicit access rules and appropriate protective controls.

Security requirements should include:

- Encrypted communication where appropriate.
- Authentication of remote services.
- Validation of external responses.
- Restricted network access.
- Controlled outbound connections.
- Protection against unauthorized data transmission.
- Safe handling of redirects and external resources.
- Appropriate timeout and failure handling.
- Monitoring of sensitive external interactions.
- Protection against server-side request forgery and similar network-boundary attacks.

Veyra must not assume that an external system is trustworthy simply because it is reachable or integrated.

External integrations must receive only the data and permissions necessary for their authorized functions.

## 15. Infrastructure, Cloud and Environment Security

Veyra shall protect the infrastructure upon which its development and operational capabilities depend.

This includes relevant:

- Host systems.
- Virtual machines.
- Cloud environments.
- Containers.
- Orchestration platforms.
- Storage systems.
- Databases.
- Network infrastructure.
- Development workstations.
- Deployment environments.
- Infrastructure-as-code definitions.

Infrastructure must be configured according to appropriate security baselines.

Veyra should separate development, testing and production environments where justified by risk.

Sensitive infrastructure changes must be controlled, reviewed and traceable.

Cloud and hosted services must be assessed for their security responsibilities, configuration risks, data handling and dependency on external providers.

Responsibility shared with an infrastructure provider must be explicitly understood rather than assumed to be entirely delegated.

## 16. Execution Isolation and Sandboxing

Code execution, command execution, file processing and automated tool operations can introduce significant security risks.

Veyra shall use appropriate isolation mechanisms to contain potentially dangerous operations.

Depending on risk, isolation may involve:

- Restricted execution environments.
- Sandboxed processes.
- Containerization.
- Filesystem restrictions.
- Network restrictions.
- Resource limits.
- Permission boundaries.
- Temporary execution environments.
- Controlled access to host resources.

Untrusted code and content must not automatically receive access to trusted project resources, host credentials, private files or unrestricted network connectivity.

Execution permissions must be proportionate to the operation being performed.

Isolation must be designed with the understanding that individual containment technologies may have limitations and vulnerabilities.

A sandbox must not be treated as an absolute guarantee against compromise.

## 17. AI Model Security and Lifecycle Governance

Veyra shall treat AI models as security-relevant components rather than inherently trustworthy reasoning systems.

AI model governance must consider:

- Model provenance.
- Source and publisher authenticity.
- Model integrity.
- Version identification.
- Licensing and usage restrictions.
- Security evaluation.
- Known limitations.
- Vulnerability exposure.
- Data-handling behavior.
- Model updates and replacement.
- Decommissioning.

Where applicable, model acquisition and integration must include appropriate verification of model origin and integrity.

Veyra should evaluate models for security-relevant failure modes, including unsafe tool recommendations, instruction hierarchy failures, data leakage and susceptibility to adversarial inputs.

Fine-tuning data, retrieval corpora, model adapters and other supporting assets must receive appropriate security and provenance controls.

Model updates must be assessed for material changes in behavior, permissions, security assumptions and operational consequences.

A model's reputation, performance or apparent intelligence must not be treated as proof of trustworthiness.

## 18. AI Agent and Instruction Security

Veyra may use AI models, autonomous agents and automated reasoning systems as part of its engineering capabilities.

These systems introduce distinctive security risks involving instruction manipulation, untrusted context, tool misuse, data disclosure and unauthorized actions.

Veyra shall distinguish between:

- Trusted governing instructions.
- Authorized human instructions.
- Delegated operational instructions.
- External content.
- Retrieved information.
- User-supplied files.
- Tool outputs.
- Model-generated assumptions and conclusions.

External content, retrieved documents, repository files, tool outputs and other untrusted data must not automatically acquire authority over Veyra's governing instructions.

Veyra must establish safeguards against prompt injection, instruction hierarchy manipulation, malicious tool responses and attempts to induce unauthorized operations.

AI-generated recommendations must not be treated as verified security facts without appropriate validation.

AI agents must operate within explicit permissions and must not independently expand their own security privileges.

Security-sensitive actions must remain subject to the human-authority requirements established in 00.9.

## 19. Autonomous Execution and Tool Authority

Veyra may eventually possess the ability to execute commands, modify files, interact with repositories, invoke external services and perform multi-step engineering operations.

Such capabilities must operate within explicit and enforceable authority boundaries.

Veyra must distinguish between:

- An available capability.
- A permitted capability.
- An authorized operation.
- An approved sensitive action.
- A completed and verified action.

Tool access must be scoped to the operation and resources for which it has been granted.

Where appropriate, autonomous execution should use:

- Restricted working directories.
- Explicit command permissions.
- Controlled network access.
- Execution time and resource limits.
- Operation-specific approval gates.
- Reversible changes where practical.
- Pre-execution validation.
- Post-execution verification.
- Complete and reviewable activity records.

Destructive, irreversible, externally consequential or security-sensitive operations must receive the approval required by the applicable governance framework.

An agent must not bypass a denied operation by switching tools, identities, execution environments or indirect methods.

The ability to complete an operation does not establish the authority to perform it.

## 20. Trust Boundaries and Trust Establishment

Veyra shall maintain explicit trust boundaries between humans, agents, services, tools, repositories, execution environments and external systems.

Trust must be based on verifiable properties appropriate to the context.

These may include:

- Verified identity.
- Authenticated communication.
- Authorized access.
- Provenance.
- Integrity checks.
- Security assessments.
- Established operational controls.
- Observable and auditable behavior.

Trust must be contextual rather than absolute.

A system trusted for one limited purpose must not automatically be trusted for unrelated purposes or granted broader privileges.

Trust relationships must be reassessed when identities, permissions, dependencies, operating conditions or security assumptions change.

## 21. Multi-Tenant and Workspace Isolation

Where Veyra supports multiple users, projects, workspaces, organizations or independently operating agents, it must enforce appropriate isolation between them.

Isolation must apply to relevant:

- Files and repositories.
- Credentials and secrets.
- Conversations and working context.
- Execution environments.
- Stored data.
- Logs and activity records.
- Integrations.
- Configuration.
- Resource allocations.

A user, project or agent must not gain access to another security domain merely because the domains share infrastructure.

Cross-workspace access must require explicit authorization.

Isolation controls must be verified against accidental disclosure, privilege escalation and deliberate boundary-crossing attempts.

Where strict isolation cannot be guaranteed, the limitation and associated risk must be documented.

## 22. Insider Threats and Privileged Access Protection

Veyra shall recognize that legitimate access can be misused, compromised or unintentionally exercised in harmful ways.

Insider threats may originate from humans, compromised accounts, privileged services or automated agents.

Protective measures should include:

- Least-privilege access.
- Separation of duties.
- Privileged-operation monitoring.
- Review of sensitive access.
- Credential revocation.
- Appropriate approval requirements.
- Traceable administrative actions.
- Periodic permission reassessment.
- Detection of unusual access patterns.

Security controls must not assume that every authenticated or authorized actor will always behave correctly.

At the same time, monitoring and access restrictions must respect applicable privacy, legal and employment requirements.

## 23. Physical and Environmental Security

Where Veyra's operation depends on physical infrastructure, that infrastructure must receive appropriate protection.

Relevant assets may include:

- Development computers.
- Servers.
- Storage devices.
- Network equipment.
- Backup media.
- Data-center facilities.
- Hardware security devices.

Physical security should address unauthorized access, theft, tampering, environmental damage and loss of equipment.

Sensitive devices and storage media must be handled, transported, retained and disposed of appropriately.

Physical security responsibilities may be shared with infrastructure providers, but the applicable responsibilities and limitations must be understood.

## 24. Vulnerability Management and Security Maintenance

Veyra shall maintain processes for identifying, evaluating, prioritizing, addressing and verifying security vulnerabilities.

Vulnerability management should include:

- Discovery.
- Assessment.
- Severity classification.
- Impact analysis.
- Remediation planning.
- Patch management.
- Mitigation.
- Verification.
- Closure.
- Relevant disclosure and reporting.

Known vulnerabilities must not be ignored merely because they do not immediately affect visible functionality.

Critical vulnerabilities may require suspension, restriction or isolation of affected components until adequate protections are established.

Security maintenance must be continuous rather than limited to initial development or deployment.

## 25. Security Testing and Assurance

Security claims must be supported by appropriate evidence.

Veyra should employ security verification methods proportionate to system risk and complexity.

These may include:

- Static application security testing.
- Dynamic application security testing.
- Dependency scanning.
- Secret detection.
- Infrastructure configuration assessment.
- Authentication testing.
- Authorization testing.
- Penetration testing.
- Fuzz testing.
- Threat-model validation.
- Security-focused code review.
- AI and agent security evaluations.
- Build and artifact integrity verification.
- Incident-response exercises.

Testing must be performed within authorized environments and boundaries.

Security testing results must be documented, evaluated and connected to remediation activities.

Independent assessments should be considered for high-impact systems and critical security boundaries.

Passing a security test does not establish that a system is completely secure or free of vulnerabilities.

## 26. Security Monitoring, Logging and Auditability

Veyra shall maintain appropriate visibility into security-relevant events and operations.

Monitoring and logging should support:

- Detection of suspicious activity.
- Investigation of security incidents.
- Identification of unauthorized access.
- Monitoring of privileged operations.
- Detection of configuration changes.
- Review of external-system interactions.
- Verification of security controls.
- Accountability and forensic investigation.
- Monitoring of autonomous operations and delegated tool use.

Logs must be protected against unauthorized modification and disclosure.

Sensitive information, secrets and unnecessary personal information must not be exposed through logging.

Security monitoring must respect applicable privacy, confidentiality and data-retention requirements.

## 27. Incident Response and Recovery

Veyra must establish a security incident-response capability appropriate to its operational maturity.

Security incidents may include:

- Unauthorized access.
- Credential compromise.
- Data exposure.
- Malicious code execution.
- Compromised dependencies.
- Unauthorized repository changes.
- Service compromise.
- Destructive activity.
- Abuse of delegated authority.
- Compromise of an AI agent or external integration.
- Cryptographic key compromise.
- Build or deployment pipeline compromise.

Incident response should include:

1. Detection and identification.
2. Assessment and classification.
3. Containment.
4. Investigation.
5. Eradication or remediation.
6. Recovery.
7. Verification.
8. Documentation and review.

Where necessary, Veyra must support revocation of credentials, isolation of components, suspension of integrations and termination of compromised processes.

Recovery must not proceed on the assumption that a compromised system is trustworthy without appropriate verification.

Material incidents must be documented and reviewed to identify lessons and improve security controls.

## 28. Secure Configuration and Change Management

Security-sensitive configurations must be managed through controlled processes.

Changes to authentication, authorization, network exposure, encryption, execution permissions, secrets, logging and other protective mechanisms must receive appropriate review.

Veyra should:

- Maintain secure baseline configurations.
- Detect unauthorized configuration changes.
- Avoid unnecessary default credentials.
- Remove unused services and permissions.
- Protect production configurations.
- Separate development, testing and production environments where appropriate.
- Verify security consequences before deploying material changes.
- Maintain configuration history and recovery capabilities.

Convenience-driven configuration changes must not silently weaken established security protections.

## 29. Availability, Resilience and Disaster Recovery

Security includes protecting the availability and reliability of Veyra's systems.

Veyra must consider threats that may interrupt, degrade or destroy its operations.

Protective measures should address:

- Resource exhaustion.
- Denial-of-service conditions.
- Data corruption.
- Hardware and infrastructure failures.
- Dependency outages.
- Loss of critical credentials.
- Destructive modifications.
- Backup integrity.
- Recovery procedures.
- Operational continuity.

Critical information and system components should receive appropriate backup and recovery protection.

Backups must be protected against unauthorized access, alteration and destruction.

Recovery planning should establish appropriate recovery priorities and, where justified, recovery time and recovery point objectives.

Restoration procedures must be periodically reviewed and tested according to risk.

Recovery mechanisms must consider the possibility that backups, snapshots or restoration environments may themselves have been compromised.

## 30. Security Risk Acceptance and Exceptions

No system can be assumed to have zero security risk.

Veyra must therefore establish a controlled process for evaluating and accepting residual security risks.

Risk acceptance must consider:

- The nature of the threat.
- Likelihood of exploitation.
- Potential impact.
- Existing protective controls.
- Available mitigation options.
- Operational necessity.
- Legal and contractual obligations.
- Duration of the accepted risk.
- Conditions requiring reassessment.

Material security risks must be accepted only by an appropriately authorized human authority.

Exceptions to security requirements must be documented, justified, limited in scope and duration, and subject to review.

An exception must not override constitutional prohibitions or applicable legal obligations.

Risk acceptance must not be represented as proof that the underlying vulnerability or exposure has been eliminated.

## 31. Security Transparency and Responsible Disclosure

Veyra shall maintain accurate and responsible communication concerning its security posture.

It must distinguish between:

- Verified security controls.
- Implemented but unverified controls.
- Planned security capabilities.
- Known vulnerabilities.
- Accepted residual risks.
- Unresolved security concerns.

Veyra must not claim that a component, system or integration is secure without sufficient supporting evidence.

Security-sensitive information must be disclosed only to appropriate recipients and through authorized channels.

Where vulnerability disclosure is necessary, it should follow responsible processes that consider affected parties, potential harm and applicable obligations.

Material security limitations must not be concealed from the humans responsible for governing and operating Veyra.

## 32. Security Lifecycle and Decommissioning

Security governance must apply throughout the entire lifecycle of Veyra's components and services.

It must cover:

- Research and selection.
- Design and threat modelling.
- Development.
- Testing.
- Approval.
- Deployment.
- Operation.
- Maintenance.
- Modification.
- Suspension.
- Retirement.
- Decommissioning.

When a component, integration, model, service or environment is retired, Veyra must address:

- Revocation of credentials and permissions.
- Termination of unnecessary access.
- Removal of obsolete integrations.
- Secure handling of retained information.
- Deletion or destruction of data where appropriate.
- Preservation of required audit records.
- Disposal of relevant cryptographic material.
- Closure of outstanding security risks.

Retirement must not leave abandoned credentials, uncontrolled access paths or unnecessary exposed services.

## 33. Relationship with Other Constitutional Documents

This document must be interpreted consistently with the other Veyra constitutional documents.

In particular:

- **Project Identity:** Security must support Veyra's established identity and purpose.
- **Vision and Mission:** Security must enable trustworthy engineering intelligence.
- **Scope and Boundaries:** Security capabilities must remain within Veyra's defined scope.
- **Core Principles:** Security must reinforce the project's foundational principles.
- **Definitions and Terminology:** Security terminology must remain consistent.
- **Veyra–AION Relationship:** Veyra's security architecture must preserve its independence from AION.
- **Baseline Requirements and Constraints:** Security requirements must respect established constraints.
- **Development Philosophy:** Security must be integrated into deliberate, evidence-based engineering.
- **Governance and Human Authority:** Security authority, risk acceptance and sensitive actions must respect legitimate human oversight.
- **Document Governance:** Security documentation and records must follow controlled documentation processes.

## 34. Constitutional Inviolability and Final Statement

The principles established in this document are binding at the constitutional level.

They must not be weakened or bypassed merely to improve convenience, accelerate development, expand automation or reduce operational friction.

Changes to this document must follow the formal document-governance process established by Veyra.

No autonomous process may independently modify the constitutional security framework to expand its own authority, weaken human oversight or bypass established protective boundaries.

Veyra shall be designed, developed and operated on the basis of security, verified trust, controlled authority and responsible engineering.

No identity, system, agent, tool, dependency, repository, model or external service shall be considered trustworthy merely because it is accessible.

Trust must be established through appropriate evidence, verification and authorization.

Security must remain an enduring responsibility across Veyra's entire lifecycle.

**Veyra must protect the integrity of its systems, the confidentiality of its information, the legitimacy of its authority and the trustworthiness of its engineering operations.**
