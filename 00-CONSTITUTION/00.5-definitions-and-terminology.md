# VEYRA — Definitions & Terminology

**Document ID:** VEYRA-00.5  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Canonical Working Draft  
**Parent:** Phase 0 — Foundation / Constitution  
**Depends On:** VEYRA-00.1 Project Identity, VEYRA-00.2 Vision & Mission, VEYRA-00.3 Scope & Boundaries, VEYRA-00.4 Core Principles

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the canonical terminology used throughout the Veyra project.

Its purpose is to reduce ambiguity between terms that may otherwise be used interchangeably.

Definitions established here apply to subsequent Veyra documentation unless a later governed document explicitly establishes a more specific technical meaning.

This document defines concepts at the constitutional level.

It does not prescribe final implementation mechanisms.

---

## 2. Veyra

Veyra is the canonical name of the general-purpose autonomous software-engineering intelligence and software-production platform being developed by this project.

Veyra is intended to coordinate the intelligence, knowledge, tools, execution, verification, operational capabilities, and governance required to support software engineering across its lifecycle.

---

## 3. AION

AION refers to the broader personal-intelligence vision associated with the overall project lineage.

AION is distinct from Veyra.

Veyra is the software-engineering intelligence/platform.

AION is a broader personal-intelligence system that Veyra may eventually help construct.

---

## 4. AION Builder

AION Builder is the historical name previously used for what is now Veyra.

The term may appear in historical documents, earlier discussions, or preserved design material.

New canonical documentation should use Veyra unless historical context requires the former name.

---

## 5. Builder Foundry

Builder Foundry refers to the development environment, methodology, tooling, and supporting engineering ecosystem used to construct Veyra itself.

The Foundry and Veyra are related but distinct concepts.

Veyra is the system being built.

The Builder Foundry is the environment and process through which it is built.

---

## 6. Software Engineering

Software engineering refers to the broader disciplined process of producing and evolving software.

Within Veyra, this includes activities such as:

- discovery;
- research;
- requirements;
- planning;
- architecture;
- design;
- implementation;
- testing;
- verification;
- debugging;
- security;
- deployment;
- operations;
- maintenance;
- evolution.

Software engineering is therefore broader than source-code generation.

---

## 7. Software-Production Lifecycle

The software-production lifecycle is the sequence of engineering activities through which software progresses from an initial idea or objective toward an operational and continuously improved system.

The constitutional lifecycle is represented as:

Idea
  ↓
Discovery
  ↓
Research
  ↓
Requirements
  ↓
Planning
  ↓
Architecture
  ↓
Design
  ↓
Implementation
  ↓
Testing
  ↓
Verification
  ↓
Debugging
  ↓
Security
  ↓
Deployment
  ↓
Operations
  ↓
Maintenance
  ↓
Evolution

This represents the intended capability domain rather than a requirement that every project follow one rigid linear sequence.

---

## 8. Capability

A capability is an ability required to perform some meaningful engineering function.

Examples include:

- reasoning;
- research;
- coding;
- architecture;
- testing;
- debugging;
- security analysis;
- deployment;
- operational analysis.

Capabilities are more fundamental than individual agents or models.

A capability may be implemented by one or multiple workers, models, tools, or subsystems.

---

## 9. Role

A role is a defined responsibility or function within an engineering workflow.

A role describes what responsibility must be performed rather than necessarily identifying who or what performs it.

A role may therefore be fulfilled by different workers or combinations of capabilities depending on project requirements.

---

## 10. Worker

A worker is an executing engineering entity assigned one or more capabilities or responsibilities.

A worker may be implemented using an AI agent, model-driven process, deterministic program, tool-assisted process, or another mechanism.

The term does not establish a specific implementation architecture.

---

## 11. Agent

An agent is an autonomous or semi-autonomous computational entity capable of performing tasks within defined objectives, context, tools, and authority.

The term is intentionally implementation-neutral at the constitutional level.

An agent may correspond to a worker, but the concepts are not necessarily identical.

Later architecture documents shall establish the precise Veyra agent model.

---

## 12. Team

A team is a coordinated collection of workers or capabilities assembled to accomplish a defined engineering objective.

A team may contain specialized responsibilities and may operate over a limited or extended period.

Teams are intended to be composable and adaptable rather than permanently fixed.

---

## 13. Department

A department is a larger organizational grouping of related engineering capabilities, roles, teams, or responsibilities.

The term is primarily organizational and conceptual at the constitutional level.

It does not require Veyra to literally reproduce a human corporate structure.

---

## 14. Engineering Organization

An engineering organization is the coordinated structure through which Veyra assembles capabilities, roles, workers, teams, and potentially departments to accomplish project objectives.

The organizational concept expresses coordinated engineering capacity.

It does not imply a fixed number of agents or workers.

---

## 15. Dynamic Workforce

A dynamic workforce is an engineering workforce whose composition can change according to project requirements, task requirements, available capabilities, constraints, and operational conditions.

Veyra is intended to use dynamic capability composition rather than depend on a permanently fixed collection of workers.

---

## 16. "100 Engineers" Concept

The "100 engineers" concept is a metaphor representing the desired scale of coordinated engineering capability within Veyra.

It is not:

- a literal requirement for one hundred agents;
- a fixed architectural limit;
- a requirement to simulate one hundred humans;
- a canonical workforce size.

The concept represents substantial coordinated engineering capacity.

---

## 17. Orchestration

Orchestration is the coordination of engineering activities, capabilities, workers, tools, information, dependencies, execution, and results toward project objectives.

Orchestration may include:

- task decomposition;
- assignment;
- scheduling;
- dependency management;
- coordination;
- state management;
- result integration;
- failure handling;
- verification coordination.

The final orchestration architecture remains subject to later design.

---

## 18. Intelligence

Intelligence, within Veyra, refers to the capability to understand, reason, research, plan, decide, adapt, diagnose, and otherwise perform cognitive functions required for software engineering.

Intelligence is not synonymous with a particular AI model.

Veyra may combine multiple intelligence resources.

---

## 19. Model

A model is an AI computational model used to provide one or more capabilities required by Veyra.

Models may include:

- frontier models;
- open-weight models;
- coding models;
- reasoning models;
- local models;
- specialized models.

The term does not establish a preferred provider or model.

---

## 20. Tool

A tool is an external or internal capability that enables Veyra to perform an operation that may not be reliably accomplished through reasoning alone.

Examples include:

- source-control systems;
- code execution;
- testing systems;
- package managers;
- research systems;
- deployment systems;
- infrastructure systems;
- observability systems.

Tool access is subject to authority and security controls.

---

## 21. Execution Environment

An execution environment is the computational environment in which software, code, tools, tests, or other operations are executed.

An execution environment may include operating-system resources, runtimes, containers, virtual machines, sandboxes, or other isolation mechanisms.

The final Veyra execution architecture remains undecided.

---

## 22. Project

A project is a software-engineering objective or body of work undertaken using Veyra.

A project may be internal to Veyra, associated with AION, or provided by an external customer.

Projects may differ in technology, scale, architecture, requirements, and operational constraints.

---

## 23. Project State

Project state is the information required to understand the current condition and history of a software project.

It may include:

- requirements;
- plans;
- tasks;
- decisions;
- source code;
- architecture;
- configuration;
- test results;
- failures;
- deployment state;
- operational information;
- project history.

---

## 24. Context

Context is the information made available to an engineering operation at a particular point in time.

Context may include project information, task information, relevant knowledge, previous results, instructions, tool outputs, and other information necessary for the operation.

Context is not necessarily persistent.

---

## 25. Memory

Memory refers to persistent or semi-persistent information retained for future use.

Memory may include project knowledge, decisions, history, learned information, or other information that should survive beyond an individual operation.

The precise Veyra memory architecture remains subject to later research and architecture.

---

## 26. Knowledge

Knowledge is information considered relevant to understanding or performing an engineering task.

Knowledge may originate from:

- project artifacts;
- research;
- documentation;
- source code;
- previous engineering work;
- external technical information.

Knowledge may have different confidence, provenance, freshness, and verification status.

---

## 27. Engineering Decision

An engineering decision is a deliberate selection between possible approaches, designs, technologies, or actions.

Important decisions should be traceable to relevant:

- requirements;
- evidence;
- constraints;
- assumptions;
- research;
- evaluations.

---

## 28. Evidence

Evidence is information that supports or challenges an engineering claim, decision, or conclusion.

Evidence may include:

- test results;
- benchmarks;
- documentation;
- experiments;
- source-code analysis;
- operational observations;
- security findings;
- research.

Model confidence alone does not constitute sufficient evidence of correctness.

---

## 29. Verification

Verification is the process of determining whether an artifact, implementation, behavior, or result satisfies applicable requirements or expectations.

Verification may include:

- tests;
- static analysis;
- type checking;
- code review;
- security analysis;
- integration testing;
- behavioral evaluation;
- deployment validation;
- runtime observation.

---

## 30. Validation

Validation is the process of determining whether the resulting system actually fulfills the intended objective or need.

Verification asks whether the implementation satisfies defined conditions.

Validation asks whether the resulting system solves the intended problem.

The precise verification and validation framework will be developed later.

---

## 31. Failure

A failure is an event in which an operation, artifact, decision, system, or outcome does not satisfy its applicable requirements or expectations.

Failures may occur in:

- reasoning;
- implementation;
- testing;
- tools;
- infrastructure;
- coordination;
- security;
- deployment;
- operations.

Failures should provide information for diagnosis and improvement where practical.

---

## 32. Closed-Loop Engineering

Closed-loop engineering is the continuous process of observing engineering results, identifying problems, diagnosing causes, applying corrections, verifying those corrections, and continuing the process.

A conceptual loop is:

Understand
    ↓
Research
    ↓
Plan
    ↓
Design
    ↓
Build
    ↓
Test
    ↓
Diagnose
    ↓
Repair
    ↓
Review
    ↓
Retest
    ↓
Deploy
    ↓
Observe
    ↓
Improve
    ↺

---

## 33. Autonomy

Autonomy is the ability of Veyra or one of its components to perform engineering activities without requiring continuous human direction.

Autonomy is bounded by:

- objectives;
- permissions;
- constraints;
- security controls;
- governance;
- human authority.

Autonomy does not mean unrestricted independence.

---

## 34. Human Authority

Human authority is the ability of authorized humans to define objectives, establish constraints, approve or reject consequential actions, intervene in execution, revoke permissions, and halt operations where appropriate.

Human authority is a constitutional governance principle of Veyra.

---

## 35. Governance

Governance is the system of rules, authorities, processes, controls, and records through which Veyra and its development are directed and constrained.

Governance applies to both:

- the construction of Veyra;
- the operation and evolution of Veyra.

---

## 36. Security Boundary

A security boundary is a defined separation across which access, authority, data, execution, or trust must be controlled.

Security boundaries may exist around:

- tools;
- agents;
- projects;
- repositories;
- execution environments;
- infrastructure;
- customer data;
- credentials;
- external services.

---

## 37. Least Privilege

Least privilege is the principle that a component or actor should receive only the permissions necessary to perform its authorized function.

Permissions should be appropriately scoped, revocable, and auditable where practical.

---

## 38. Research

Research is the deliberate investigation of information, alternatives, technologies, evidence, or unknowns relevant to an engineering objective.

Within Veyra, research is a core software-engineering capability rather than merely an optional preliminary activity.

---

## 39. Requirement

A requirement is a condition, capability, constraint, or property that a project or system is expected to satisfy.

Requirements may originate from:

- project objectives;
- users;
- stakeholders;
- technical constraints;
- security needs;
- operational needs;
- governance.

---

## 40. Architecture

Architecture is the structured organization of a system's major components, responsibilities, relationships, boundaries, and interactions.

At the constitutional level, the term does not prescribe a specific architectural style.

---

## 41. Artifact

An artifact is a persistent output or representation produced or used during engineering.

Examples include:

- source code;
- documentation;
- specifications;
- architecture descriptions;
- configuration;
- test results;
- build outputs;
- deployment definitions;
- decision records.

---

## 42. Self-Improvement

Self-improvement is the controlled modification or enhancement of Veyra's capabilities, processes, knowledge, tools, or software.

Self-improvement must remain subject to appropriate:

- authority;
- verification;
- testing;
- observability;
- security;
- reversibility where practical.

---

## 43. General-Purpose

General-purpose, in the context of Veyra, means capable of adapting its software-engineering capabilities across diverse projects, domains, technologies, architectures, and operational requirements rather than being optimized exclusively for one project.

General-purpose does not mean unlimited capability.

---

## 44. Model Independence

Model independence is the architectural objective of avoiding unnecessary dependence on one particular AI model or provider.

It does not prohibit using a particular model where evidence demonstrates that doing so is advantageous.

---

## 45. Technology Independence

Technology independence is the objective of avoiding unnecessary dependence on one particular programming language, framework, database, cloud provider, operating system, or infrastructure technology.

Technology choices remain subject to later research and architectural decisions.

---

## 46. Customer Mode

Customer Mode refers to operation of Veyra as a service or platform for external users and their software projects.

Customer Mode requires appropriate isolation of:

- customer projects;
- customer data;
- customer permissions;
- customer environments;
- customer operational state.

---

## 47. Owner / Developer Mode

Owner / Developer Mode refers to operation of Veyra for constructing, evaluating, operating, maintaining, and improving Veyra itself.

This mode may have capabilities that are inappropriate for ordinary customer environments.

---

## 48. Development Scope

Development Scope refers to the capabilities that can realistically be built, tested, and operated at the current stage of Veyra's development.

Development scope is constrained by available:

- compute;
- models;
- tools;
- infrastructure;
- knowledge;
- engineering capacity;
- budget;
- testing capability.

---

## 49. Ultimate Capability Scope

Ultimate Capability Scope refers to the broader set of capabilities Veyra is intended to achieve if the relevant technical and engineering challenges can be solved.

Ultimate capability scope must not be confused with capabilities that currently exist.

---

## 50. Constitutional Document

A constitutional document is a Phase 0 document establishing stable project identity, principles, boundaries, governance, terminology, or foundational constraints.

Constitutional documents guide later research, requirements, architecture, implementation, and operations.

---

## 51. Master Design Order

The Master Design Order is the governing sequence through which Veyra is to be systematically developed.

It establishes the relationship and progression between foundational definition, challenge identification, research, requirements, architecture, implementation, testing, operations, and evolution.

The canonical sequence is established separately in "00.12-master-design-order.md".

---

## 52. Canonical

Canonical means that a document, definition, decision, or artifact has been formally adopted as the authoritative project reference for its applicable subject.

Canonical status does not mean that the subject can never change.

A canonical item may be superseded through the project's governance process.

---

## 53. Working Document

A working document is a project document that is actively used and may be refined as the project develops.

A working document remains subject to the project's document governance and versioning rules.

---

## 54. Superseded

Superseded means that a previous document, decision, definition, or artifact has been replaced by a later governed version.

Superseded material should remain traceable where historically or technically useful.

It should not be silently erased merely because it is no longer current.

---

## 55. Core Terminology Principle

The canonical terminology principle is:

«Veyra shall use terminology consistently enough that architectural, engineering, governance, and research decisions can be understood without relying on hidden assumptions or ambiguous language.»

Where a term acquires a more precise technical meaning during later architecture or implementation, the more specific definition shall be explicitly documented rather than silently changing the constitutional meaning.

---

## 56. Status

This document establishes the working vocabulary for the Veyra project.

Definitions may be refined as research and architecture establish more precise technical meanings.

Material changes to foundational terminology shall be explicitly documented and governed.

---

## 57. Summary

The purpose of Veyra's terminology is not to create terminology for its own sake.

The vocabulary exists to provide a common language for:

- software engineering;
- intelligence;
- capabilities;
- workers;
- teams;
- orchestration;
- tools;
- execution;
- context;
- memory;
- knowledge;
- verification;
- security;
- governance.
