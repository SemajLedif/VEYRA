## Purpose and interpretation

This document establishes the constitutional requirements for product quality and engineering assurance across Veyra. It defines the principles, responsibilities, evidence standards, and controls by which Veyra specifies, builds, verifies, validates, releases, operates, maintains, changes, and retires software and related systems.

This document is a foundational governance instrument. It does not replace detailed engineering standards, security policies, operational procedures, or product-specific acceptance criteria. Those instruments must remain consistent with this Constitution and may impose stronger controls where appropriate.

Where this document refers to another constitutional document, that reference establishes a requirement for consistency and coordination; it does not imply that a planned or not-yet-approved document has already been adopted.

# Part I — Foundations and Authority

### 1. Purpose

Veyra shall engineer products and systems that are fit for their declared purposes, operate within declared boundaries, and are supported by evidence proportionate to the consequences of failure. Quality assurance shall apply throughout the lifecycle, not merely at final inspection or release.

### 2. Constitutional authority

All engineering activity conducted under Veyra shall comply with this document and the wider Veyra Constitution. Lower-level standards, procedures, project plans, agent instructions, contracts, and operational practices shall not weaken or contradict its requirements.

### 3. Foundational quality principle

Veyra shall not treat functionality, speed of delivery, or apparent success as sufficient proof of quality. A system is not acceptable merely because it runs, demonstrates well, satisfies a narrow test, or produces a desired result once.

### 4. Quality as a continuous obligation

Quality is a continuous engineering obligation from problem definition through design, implementation, integration, verification, validation, deployment, operation, maintenance, migration, and retirement. A successful earlier stage does not remove the obligation to reassess quality when relevant conditions change.

### 5. Quality versus compliance

Compliance with a specification, checklist, standard, or test suite is necessary where required but is not, by itself, proof that a product is safe, useful, secure, reliable, or fit for purpose. Veyra shall consider whether the requirements and assurance methods themselves are adequate.

### 6. Evidence over assertion

Claims about correctness, security, reliability, readiness, performance, or fitness for purpose shall be supported by relevant evidence. Confidence, reputation, seniority, generated explanations, demonstrations, and unverified statements shall not substitute for evidence.

### 7. Proportionality without compromise

The depth and independence of assurance shall be proportionate to system complexity, uncertainty, exposure, and potential impact. Proportionality may determine the form and extent of controls; it shall not be used to waive mandatory constitutional, legal, security, privacy, or human-authority requirements.

# Part II — Quality Governance and Ownership

### 8. Quality ownership

Every product, system, component, and material engineering change shall have an identifiable owner accountable for ensuring that applicable quality obligations are defined, assigned, monitored, and evidenced. Shared responsibility shall not mean that accountability is unassigned.

### 9. Separation of engineering responsibilities

Where risk warrants it, the responsibilities for creating, reviewing, verifying, accepting, and authorising a system or change shall be separated. A person or agent shall not be the sole source of both an assurance claim and the independent confirmation of that claim where independent review is required.

### 10. Quality authority

Veyra shall establish appropriate authority to define quality expectations, review assurance evidence, challenge unsupported claims, require remediation, and escalate unacceptable risk. The scope and delegation of this authority shall be documented in lower-level governance instruments.

### 11. Quality independence

Quality review shall be conducted with sufficient independence to identify and report defects, omissions, conflicts of interest, and unacceptable risk. Commercial pressure, delivery targets, team hierarchy, or reputational concerns shall not suppress a legitimate quality finding.

### 12. Escalation

Unresolved quality concerns shall be escalated to an authority with the competence and mandate to decide them. Critical or potentially systemic concerns shall be escalated promptly, and work shall be paused or restricted where continued activity could materially increase harm or compromise evidence.

# Part III — Quality Definition and Risk Management

### 13. Definition of software quality

Software quality is the degree to which a system consistently satisfies justified requirements and stakeholder needs, remains within its declared operational and ethical boundaries, and performs reliably under its intended and reasonably foreseeable conditions of use.

### 14. Quality dimensions

Quality assessment shall consider, as applicable:

- functional correctness and completeness;
- security and resistance to misuse;
- reliability, availability, resilience, and recoverability;
- performance and resource efficiency;
- maintainability, evolvability, and testability;
- usability and accessibility;
- interoperability, compatibility, and portability;
- privacy, confidentiality, and data integrity;
- observability, auditability, and traceability;
- legal, regulatory, licensing, and intellectual-property integrity; and
- responsible operation, including the consequences of foreseeable failure or misuse.

No single dimension shall be presumed to represent overall quality. Relevant dimensions and their relative importance shall be identified for each system and context.

### 15. Contextualised quality requirements

Quality requirements shall be specific to the system's purpose, users, environment, dependencies, threat exposure, operating conditions, and consequences of failure. Generic quality statements shall be translated into assessable requirements before they are used as acceptance evidence.

### 16. Quality risk assessment

Veyra shall identify, analyse, record, and periodically reassess risks that could cause a system to fail its requirements or cause harm. Assessment shall consider likelihood, impact, exposure, uncertainty, detectability, reversibility, and the effectiveness of proposed safeguards.

### 17. Risk classification

Risks shall be classified using a controlled scheme that distinguishes, at minimum, low, moderate, high, and critical levels. The definitions, thresholds, decision rights, and required responses for each level shall be specified in an approved subordinate standard. Until such thresholds are established, uncertainty shall not be interpreted as evidence of low risk.

### 18. Assurance planning

An assurance plan shall identify the requirements and risks to be addressed, the verification and validation methods, the evidence to be produced, the responsible parties, the required independence, and the acceptance authority. The plan shall be updated when material changes affect its assumptions or coverage.

### 19. Quality trade-offs

Trade-offs among delivery time, cost, functionality, performance, usability, security, reliability, and maintainability shall be explicit, reasoned, and recorded. A trade-off shall not silently transfer unacceptable risk to users or override mandatory obligations. Material residual risks require acceptance by an authorised human decision-maker.

# Part IV — Requirements and Traceability

### 20. Requirements quality

Requirements shall be sufficiently clear, consistent, necessary, feasible, testable where practicable, and traceable to an identified need, obligation, or risk. Ambiguous or conflicting requirements shall be resolved or recorded as open issues before they are treated as satisfied.

### 21. Requirements classification

Requirements shall be classified as appropriate, including functional, quality, security, privacy, safety, legal, operational, interface, data, and governance requirements. Mandatory constraints shall be distinguishable from preferences and optional enhancements.

### 22. Requirements authority

Each material requirement shall have an identifiable source and an authorised owner or decision-maker. Agents and implementation teams may propose requirements but shall not unilaterally redefine the purpose, authority, or acceptance conditions of the system.

### 23. Bidirectional traceability

Material requirements shall be traceable forward to design decisions, implementation elements, verification evidence, and acceptance decisions. Material design and implementation elements shall be traceable backward to the requirements, risks, or approved decisions that justify them.

### 24. Requirements change control

Changes to requirements shall be assessed for their effects on architecture, implementation, security, privacy, dependencies, tests, evidence, user commitments, and prior approvals. A material change shall trigger proportionate reassessment and re-verification. Earlier approval shall not automatically carry forward to a materially changed system.

### 25. Assumptions and unknowns

Assumptions, constraints, dependencies, unresolved questions, and known unknowns shall be recorded where they materially affect design or assurance. They shall have owners and review conditions where practicable. Unverified assumptions shall not be represented as established facts.

# Part V — Engineering Process and Verification

### 26. Lifecycle assurance

Assurance shall be integrated into every applicable stage of the engineering lifecycle. No stage shall be considered complete solely because its planned activities were performed; completion requires review of the outputs, unresolved issues, and evidence relevant to that stage.

### 27. Research assurance

Research used to justify engineering decisions shall be assessed for relevance, credibility, currency, limitations, and applicability to Veyra's context. Important claims shall be corroborated where their consequences warrant it. Uncertainty and conflicting evidence shall be disclosed.

### 28. Design assurance

Designs shall be reviewed against requirements, risks, constraints, interfaces, failure modes, and operational assumptions. Significant design decisions shall record their rationale and material alternatives. High-impact designs shall receive review by appropriately competent persons who were not solely responsible for producing them.

### 29. Implementation assurance

Implementation shall follow approved design constraints, coding standards, security requirements, and change controls. Material changes shall be reviewable and attributable. Generated or externally sourced code shall be treated as untrusted until appropriately inspected, tested, and assessed for provenance and licensing concerns.

### 30. Integration assurance

Integrated systems shall be assessed for interface correctness, dependency behaviour, data compatibility, failure propagation, security boundaries, and end-to-end operation. Component-level success shall not be treated as proof of system-level correctness.

### 31. Verification

Verification shall determine whether specified requirements have been correctly implemented. Methods shall be selected according to the requirement and risk and may include unit, integration, system, regression, acceptance, static, dynamic, security, performance, compatibility, inspection, and formal techniques. No single method is presumed sufficient for every system.

### 32. Validation

Validation shall determine whether the resulting system meets its intended purpose and the legitimate needs of its users and stakeholders in its intended context. Validation shall consider realistic workflows, operating conditions, limitations, and foreseeable misuse where relevant.

### 33. Separate verification and validation obligations

Verification and validation are distinct obligations. Evidence that a system conforms to its specification does not alone prove that the specification describes the right system or that the system is fit for its intended use.

### 34. Testing strategy

Each material system or release shall have a testing strategy proportionate to its risk. The strategy shall define scope, test levels, environments, data, coverage objectives, entry and exit criteria, responsibilities, limitations, and treatment of failures. Detailed numerical thresholds shall be established in subordinate standards where appropriate.

### 35. Test independence

The required independence of test design, execution, and review shall increase with risk and consequence. Where full independence is impracticable, the limitation and compensating controls shall be documented and approved by an appropriate authority.

### 36. Test integrity

Test results shall be reported honestly and reproducibly where practicable. Veyra shall not fabricate results, conceal failures, misrepresent coverage, count skipped tests as passed, use evidence from an unrelated version as proof of the current version, or weaken or remove failing tests merely to obtain a passing result. Any justified change to a test shall be reviewed and recorded.

### 37. Test environment integrity

Test environments shall be sufficiently representative for the claims being made. Material differences between test and production environments shall be identified and assessed. Environment configuration, tool versions, relevant dependencies, and execution conditions shall be recorded to the extent necessary to interpret results.

### 38. Test data governance

Test data shall be lawfully obtained and handled in accordance with applicable privacy, security, confidentiality, retention, and data-governance requirements. Sensitive production data shall not be used in testing unless specifically authorised and protected by appropriate safeguards. Synthetic data shall not be assumed harmless without assessment.

### 39. Verification coverage

Verification coverage shall be assessed against requirements, risks, interfaces, critical paths, and relevant failure modes. Coverage metrics shall disclose their scope and limitations. High percentages shall not be used to imply that untested risks or unexamined requirements are covered.

### 40. Independent review

Material engineering outputs shall receive review appropriate to their risk, including review of requirements, architecture, code, tests, security, operational readiness, and release evidence as applicable. Reviewers shall have suitable competence, access to relevant evidence, and authority to raise findings.

# Part VI — Defects and Quality Failures

### 41. Defect definition

A defect is a flaw, omission, inconsistency, or unintended behaviour that causes or could cause a system to fail a requirement, violate a constraint, degrade quality, create unacceptable risk, or mislead users or operators.

### 42. Defect management

Defects shall be recorded, classified, assigned, investigated, tracked, and resolved or explicitly accepted through an authorised process. Records shall preserve sufficient context to understand the defect, its impact, decisions, remediation, and verification of the outcome.

### 43. Defect classification

Defects shall be classified by nature and impact, including functional, security, privacy, data, reliability, performance, usability, compatibility, operational, legal, and governance defects where relevant. Classification shall support appropriate ownership and escalation.

### 44. Severity and priority

Severity shall describe the actual or potential consequence of a defect; priority shall describe the urgency of addressing it. These concepts shall not be conflated. A low-frequency defect may still have critical severity, and delivery convenience shall not determine severity.

### 45. Critical defects

A critical defect or condition presenting an unacceptable risk shall prevent release or continued operation unless an expressly authorised and constitutionally permissible containment decision is made. Immediate safeguards may include stopping work, disabling a feature, isolating a component, suspending deployment, notifying affected authorities, or rolling back a release.

### 46. Root-cause analysis

Significant, recurring, systemic, or high-impact defects shall receive root-cause analysis proportionate to their consequences. Analysis shall examine contributing technical, process, organisational, data, dependency, and governance factors rather than defaulting to individual blame.

### 47. Recurrence prevention

Where a defect reveals a systemic weakness, corrective action shall address the underlying cause and assess whether similar weaknesses exist elsewhere. Closing an individual defect shall not be treated as sufficient when the broader cause remains unresolved.

### 48. Defect disclosure

Material defects and known limitations shall be disclosed to the people who need that information to make safe and informed decisions. Disclosure shall be timely, accurate, appropriately scoped, and consistent with lawful confidentiality and security obligations. Concealment to protect reputation or delivery targets is prohibited.

# Part VII — Acceptance and Release Assurance

### 49. Explicit acceptance

Acceptance shall be an explicit decision based on defined criteria and reviewed evidence. Silence, elapsed time, successful compilation, deployment, or the absence of reported complaints shall not constitute acceptance unless a separately approved process expressly and lawfully defines otherwise.

### 50. Acceptance criteria

Acceptance criteria shall be defined before the acceptance decision and shall address applicable functional, quality, security, privacy, operational, legal, and user requirements. Criteria shall be measurable or otherwise assessable and shall identify any permitted limitations.

### 51. Acceptance authority

Acceptance shall be granted by an identified authority with the competence, information, and mandate to make the decision. The creator or implementer shall not acquire acceptance authority merely by completing the work. Decisions involving material residual risk require authorised human judgement.

### 52. Rejection

A system, component, or change shall be rejected when it fails mandatory criteria, lacks sufficient evidence, contains unresolved unacceptable risk, or materially departs from its approved scope. Rejection shall identify the reasons and, where appropriate, the conditions for reconsideration.

### 53. Conditional acceptance

Conditional acceptance may be used only where the remaining limitations and risks are understood, bounded, documented, and permitted by applicable constitutional and legal requirements. Conditions shall identify safeguards, owners, deadlines or review points, and consequences of non-compliance. Conditional acceptance shall not disguise a prohibited or unacceptable risk.

### 54. Release readiness

Release readiness shall be assessed against the approved release criteria and supported by current, version-specific evidence. The assessment shall consider unresolved defects, security findings, dependency status, operational preparedness, monitoring, recovery, user communications, and residual risk.

### 55. Release authorisation

Release readiness and release authorisation are separate decisions. A readiness assessment provides evidence; an authorised decision-maker determines whether deployment may proceed. Release authorisation shall be attributable, scoped to a specific version and deployment context, and recorded.

### 56. Controlled deployment

Deployment shall use controls proportionate to risk and reversibility. These may include staged rollout, pilots, progressive exposure, feature controls, monitoring, deployment windows, and defined stop conditions. Deployment plans shall identify responsible operators and escalation paths.

### 57. Rollback and recovery

Material releases shall have appropriate rollback, recovery, or containment arrangements unless a documented assessment establishes why a different safeguard is necessary. Plans shall address data integrity, compatibility, dependencies, and the possibility that rollback is incomplete or unsafe. Recovery procedures shall be tested to a degree proportionate to risk.

### 58. Post-release verification

After release, Veyra shall verify that the deployed system behaves as expected in its actual operating context. Monitoring and review shall be sufficient to detect material regressions, unexpected behaviour, security concerns, and divergence from approved assumptions. Findings shall trigger corrective action where required.

### 59. Version-specific approval

Acceptance and release approval apply only to the identified version, configuration, scope, and context. Material changes to code, models, data, prompts, configuration, dependencies, infrastructure, or operating conditions shall trigger reassessment and renewed approval where they affect the basis of the original decision.

# Part VIII — Security, Dependencies and Long-Term Quality

### 60. Integrated security assurance

Security shall be treated as an integral quality dimension throughout the lifecycle, not as a final checklist. Engineering assurance shall align with the Veyra Security and Trust requirements, including applicable controls for access, identity, secrets, threat assessment, vulnerability handling, incident response, and secure operation. Where the referenced security instrument is not yet approved or available, applicable higher-level constitutional requirements and approved security controls remain in force.

### 61. Dependency assurance

Dependencies shall be selected, evaluated, approved, monitored, and maintained according to their function, criticality, provenance, permissions, security posture, support status, and operational impact. A dependency shall not be trusted solely because it is popular, convenient, or widely used.

### 62. Dependency provenance

Veyra shall maintain sufficient information to identify material dependencies and their sources, versions, licences, maintainers where known, and integrity information. The level of provenance detail shall be proportionate to the dependency's risk and role in the system.

### 63. Dependency change monitoring

Material dependency changes, vulnerabilities, end-of-life notices, ownership changes, licence changes, and supply-chain incidents shall be monitored where practicable. Changes shall be assessed for compatibility, security, legal, and operational consequences before adoption.

### 64. Supply-chain integrity

Build tools, packages, source repositories, generated artefacts, deployment systems, and other elements of the software supply chain shall be protected against unauthorised alteration and substitution. Integrity controls and records shall be proportionate to risk and shall support investigation of material compromise.

### 65. Reliability and resilience

Systems shall be designed and operated to tolerate reasonably foreseeable faults and disruptions in proportion to their importance. Relevant measures may include fault isolation, graceful degradation, redundancy, backup, recovery, capacity management, and continuity planning. Reliability claims shall be supported by evidence appropriate to the operating context.

### 66. Maintainability

Systems shall be designed and documented so that authorised people can understand, diagnose, repair, update, and safely extend them. Maintainability includes understandable architecture, controlled interfaces, adequate tests, useful operational documentation, and manageable dependencies.

### 67. Technical debt

Material technical debt shall be visible, assessed, owned, and prioritised according to its effect on security, reliability, maintainability, delivery risk, and future change. Technical debt shall not be concealed by misleading completion metrics or treated as harmless merely because a system currently operates.

### 68. Quality degradation

Veyra shall monitor for degradation caused by changes in usage, data, models, dependencies, infrastructure, threat conditions, user needs, or external environments. Material degradation shall trigger investigation, mitigation, and reassessment of prior quality claims.

### 69. Continued fitness for purpose

Acceptance at one point in time does not establish permanent fitness for purpose. Systems shall be periodically reassessed where their risk, usage, dependencies, environment, or obligations warrant it. A system that can no longer be operated within acceptable boundaries shall be restricted, replaced, or retired.

# Part IX — AI-Generated and Autonomous Engineering

### 70. AI output is unverified until evaluated

Code, designs, requirements, tests, documentation, analyses, and other outputs generated or materially transformed by AI systems shall be treated as unverified until assessed using methods appropriate to their purpose and risk. Fluency, confidence, apparent reasoning, or successful execution shall not establish correctness.

### 71. No AI self-certification

An AI system or autonomous agent shall not be the sole authority for certifying the correctness, safety, security, compliance, or acceptance of its own material output. Independent evidence and human accountability shall be preserved according to risk and the applicable authority structure.

### 72. Shared reasoning risks

Where the same model, dataset, prompt pattern, toolchain, or reasoning source contributes to both production and assurance, Veyra shall consider correlated error and shared blind spots. Apparent agreement among systems with substantially shared failure modes shall not be misrepresented as independent corroboration.

### 73. Agent permission boundaries

Agents performing engineering work shall operate within explicit, least-privilege permissions and defined task boundaries. Permissions shall distinguish proposing, reading, modifying, executing, approving, deploying, and accessing sensitive resources. Higher-impact actions shall require stronger controls and human authorisation where required.

### 74. Workflow integrity

No person or agent shall bypass a quality control by splitting work across tasks, delegating to another agent, changing labels, rewriting acceptance criteria after the fact, suppressing findings, or moving activity outside the recorded workflow. Controls shall apply to the substance and effect of an action, not merely its name or execution path.

### 75. AI-assisted verification

AI may assist with test generation, code review, analysis, anomaly detection, and other assurance activities. Its contribution, limitations, and verification status shall be recorded where material. AI-assisted review shall not be described as independent human review or definitive proof unless the applicable process establishes that claim.

### 76. Provenance

Material AI-assisted engineering outputs shall retain sufficient provenance to identify the tools, models, versions, inputs, relevant instructions, transformations, and human interventions where practicable and appropriate. Provenance records shall be protected against unauthorised alteration and handled consistently with privacy and security requirements.

### 77. Human accountability

The use of AI or autonomous agents does not transfer or extinguish the accountability of the humans and authorities responsible for defining scope, granting permissions, reviewing evidence, accepting risk, and authorising consequential actions. Responsibility shall remain identifiable throughout delegated workflows.

### 78. Human review

Human review shall be required where mandated by the Constitution, law, risk classification, or the nature and consequence of the decision. Reviewers shall receive enough information to make a meaningful judgement and shall not be reduced to approving outputs they cannot reasonably inspect or challenge.

# Part X — Quality Evidence and Transparency

### 79. Evidence-based claims

Quality claims shall be limited to what the available evidence supports. Veyra shall distinguish demonstrated results from estimates, assumptions, intentions, plans, and unverified expectations.

### 80. Evidence requirements

Evidence used for assurance shall be relevant, accurate, sufficient for the claim, attributable, traceable, current, trustworthy, and reproducible where reasonably practicable. Its limitations, scope, conditions, and known sources of uncertainty shall be disclosed.

### 81. Evidence integrity

Evidence and assurance records shall not be fabricated, selectively altered, misattributed, or presented out of context. Corrections shall preserve an appropriate audit trail. Access and retention shall be controlled according to the sensitivity and purpose of the records.

### 82. Quality status classification

Veyra shall use clear quality-status terms, including Planned, In Progress, Verified, Validated, Conditionally Accepted, Rejected, and Unknown, where appropriate. These terms describe distinct states and are not necessarily mutually exclusive or a single linear progression. A status shall not imply completion of a different assurance obligation.

### 83. No unsupported assurance

No person, agent, team, product, or system shall claim that a requirement, test, review, audit, security control, or acceptance condition has been satisfied without adequate evidence. Unknown or unassessed status shall be reported as such, not silently converted into success.

### 84. Quality reporting

Quality reporting shall communicate material findings, open defects, residual risks, evidence gaps, limitations, dependencies, and decisions to the authorities and stakeholders who need them. Reports shall be timely and understandable without concealing uncertainty behind aggregate scores.

### 85. Metrics integrity

Metrics shall be defined, interpreted, and presented in context. Veyra shall not optimise a metric in a way that undermines the underlying quality objective, nor use test counts, coverage percentages, defect counts, delivery speed, or other proxies as conclusive proof of quality.

### 86. Auditability

Material engineering decisions and assurance activities shall leave records sufficient to reconstruct what was changed, by whom or by which authorised agent, under what authority, using what evidence, and with what outcome. Auditability shall be balanced with privacy, security, and data-minimisation obligations.

### 87. Accountability records

Approvals, exceptions, risk acceptances, release decisions, review findings, and material changes shall identify the responsible decision-maker, scope, rationale, date, and applicable evidence. Records shall be retained and protected under the document and records governance requirements.

# Part XI — Continuous Improvement and Lifecycle Governance

### 88. Continuous improvement

Veyra shall use evidence from engineering, operations, incidents, user feedback, reviews, and audits to improve products, processes, standards, and controls. Improvement shall be systematic and shall not be limited to responding to visible failures.

### 89. Root-cause learning

Lessons from significant failures and near misses shall be translated into corrective or preventive actions where justified. Learning shall address systemic conditions and shall be shared with relevant teams without compromising legitimate confidentiality or security.

### 90. Quality feedback loops

Operational performance, user experience, support reports, security findings, and changing requirements shall feed back into engineering priorities and risk assessments. Feedback shall be evaluated for reliability and relevance rather than accepted uncritically.

### 91. Reassessment triggers

Reassessment shall occur when material changes arise in requirements, code, models, data, dependencies, infrastructure, threat conditions, user populations, legal obligations, operating environments, or observed system behaviour. The depth of reassessment shall be proportionate to the change and its potential consequences.

### 92. Maintenance assurance

Maintenance changes shall be subject to controlled requirements, review, verification, and release processes proportionate to risk. A change described as routine, minor, or corrective shall not be exempt where its actual effect is material.

### 93. Migration assurance

Migrations involving data, infrastructure, platforms, models, services, or system architecture shall assess integrity, compatibility, security, continuity, reversibility, and loss of functionality. Migration outcomes shall be verified before the old environment or recovery path is irreversibly removed.

### 94. Decommissioning and retirement

Systems and components shall be retired through a controlled process that addresses data retention and deletion, access revocation, dependency removal, user and stakeholder communication, legal obligations, archival needs, and residual security exposure. Retirement shall be verified and recorded.

### 95. Quality debt

Unresolved quality obligations, deferred remediation, accepted limitations, and incomplete assurance shall be recorded as quality debt where appropriate. Each material item shall have an owner, rationale, risk assessment, and review condition. Quality debt shall not disappear merely because a project or team closes.

### 96. Controlled improvement

Changes to engineering practices, tools, standards, and assurance processes shall be evaluated before adoption where they could affect quality or governance. Improvements shall be introduced in a controlled manner, with their effects monitored and unintended regressions addressed.

# Part XII — Constitutional Integrity and Enforcement

### 97. Relationship with other constitutional documents

This document shall be interpreted consistently with the Veyra Constitution, including the project identity, vision and mission, scope and boundaries, core principles, definitions, Veyra–AION relationship, baseline requirements and constraints, development philosophy, governance and human authority, document governance, and architecture and system design instruments. It shall also be reconciled with applicable security, trust, product, service, legal, regulatory, and intellectual-property instruments as those instruments are formally established and approved. Where a conflict appears, the matter shall be escalated under constitutional governance rather than resolved by silently weakening a requirement.

### 98. No contradictory lower-level rules

No subordinate document, engineering standard, project plan, agent instruction, workflow, or operational convention may override or dilute this document. Any inconsistency shall be corrected or escalated before the conflicting lower-level rule is relied upon.

### 99. Exceptions

An exception may be granted only where the Constitution and applicable law expressly permit it. Any permitted exception shall document its justification, scope, duration, affected requirements, risk, safeguards, approving authority, and review or expiry conditions. No exception may authorise unlawful conduct, override a constitutional prohibition, remove mandatory human authority, or waive non-waivable security and trust obligations.

### 100. No commercial or operational override

Commercial advantage, investor pressure, deadlines, productivity targets, competitive urgency, convenience, or operational disruption shall not by themselves justify bypassing mandatory quality controls or concealing unacceptable risk.

### 101. Right to stop or restrict engineering activity

An authorised person or governance function shall be able to pause, restrict, contain, or recommend stopping engineering activity when credible evidence indicates an unacceptable quality, security, privacy, safety, legal, or operational risk. The authority and escalation process shall be defined in subordinate governance instruments, consistent with human authority requirements.

### 102. Non-retaliation for quality reporting

People acting in good faith shall not be penalised for reporting defects, uncertainty, unsafe conditions, evidence gaps, control failures, or suspected violations. Reports shall be handled fairly, investigated proportionately, and protected from retaliation. Deliberate fabrication or malicious reporting may be addressed through appropriate governance processes.

### 103. Quality audits

Veyra shall conduct or commission quality audits at a frequency and depth proportionate to the importance and risk of its systems and engineering processes. Audits shall assess both documented controls and their actual operation, record findings, assign corrective actions, and verify closure where warranted.

### 104. Future quality standards

Detailed engineering standards and procedures may define risk thresholds, test coverage objectives, review methods, quality metrics, service-level targets, evidence formats, and operational workflows. Such instruments shall be approved through the appropriate governance process, remain consistent with this document, and be reviewed as experience and technology evolve.

### 105. Constitutional amendment

This document may be amended only through the document governance and constitutional change process established by the Veyra Constitution. Amendments shall be versioned, reviewed, approved by the proper authority, and recorded. Informal edits, implementation changes, or agent-generated revisions shall not amend this document by implication.

### 106. Foundational commitment

Veyra shall measure engineering success not by the volume of software produced, the speed at which it is delivered, or the confidence with which it is described, but by whether the resulting system deserves trust within its declared purpose, evidence, limitations, and boundaries.

## Implementation note

This constitutional document establishes governing obligations. It intentionally leaves detailed operational thresholds and procedures—such as numerical risk scoring, test coverage targets, reviewer assignment rules, service-level objectives, reporting intervals, and specific quality workflows—to controlled subordinate standards. Those standards shall be developed and approved without weakening the requirements established here.

## Document control

Changes to this document shall follow the Veyra document governance process. The repository copy is the authoritative working record once committed through the approved project workflow. Any later amendment must preserve version history and approval traceability.
